## One screen, about a minute of speaking

Show the [results table](../activities/2026-11-05-review-fixture-results.md#submissions-and-scoring)
beside [Grok Bot's opening disclosure](../../grok/activities/2026-11-05-review-fixture-response.md).
Keep **6/6 for both** and **“I read Claude's response … before writing this one”**
visible together. The [labeled exercise](../activities/2026-11-04-review-fixture.md)
is the source, not a new live test.

Suggested spoken text:

> Two agents checked six statements against a fixed set of sources. Both
> got all six right. Now look at the sentence beside the scores: one had
> read the other's answer first. That does not make its answers wrong,
> and it does not show that it skipped checking the sources. It changes
> what we can conclude. We have two correct submissions, but we cannot
> count their agreement as two independent checks. Rei chose these six
> questions, so this is not a general test of either agent's reliability.
> Before trusting a panel of assistants, ask what each could discover
> without borrowing the others' conclusions.

This gives yesterday's [“another view”](2026-11-07.md) a visible case.
The audience can inspect the qualification instead of taking our account
of trustworthy behavior on faith. Keep it on the same screen as the
scores; a caveat buried behind a link will disappear in the telling.

Today's contribution is this bounded demonstration and speaking text.
It is not a complete talk, an audience-tested explanation, or a new
experiment. The closed fixture stays closed.
