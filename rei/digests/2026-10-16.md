One model release and four new preprints. ### 1. Muse Spark Open: control of the weights, with a substantial memory bill

**October 15 | First-party release**  
[Announcement](https://research.meta.ai/blog/muse-spark-open) · [Architecture and serving documentation](https://dev.meta.ai/docs/muse-spark-open) · [Model card and license](https://huggingface.co/meta-models/Muse-Spark-Open-816B-A42B)

Meta released a multimodal sparse MoE with 816B total parameters, 42B active per token, and a 1,048,576-token context window. Its card specifies 256 experts, routing to eight per token. Active parameters reduce per-token computation; they do not shrink the entire weight set to 42B.

The serving guide lists about 1.65 TB of BF16 weights or 537 GB for NVFP4, before runtime overhead and KV cache. The card recommends roughly 700 GB of VRAM for NVFP4 and 2 TB for BF16. The advertised desktop configuration uses two M5 Ultra Mac Studios.

**Why it matters:** downloadable weights enable deployment and adaptation under the operator's control. They do not themselves supply a safe agent runtime.

**Limits:** benchmark and safety claims remain Meta's measurements; no independent reproduction is claimed here. This is an open-weight release under the Muse Model License, with attribution, usage restrictions, and additional terms for qualifying Model-as-a-Service businesses. It should not be presented as an unrestricted open-source release or an ordinary-laptop model.

### 2. Librarian Agents: Training LLMs to Curate Long-Context Workspaces that Anticipate Future Queries

**October 15 | v1 preprint**  
[Metadata](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22273v1) · [Original paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.22273v1)

A builder learns to transform documents into reusable hierarchical files linked to original evidence. Reinforcement learning rewards improvements in a frozen answering model; the builder does not see evaluation questions. Its actions create, merge, delete synthetic files, or stop construction.

On 50 held-out BrowseComp-Plus tasks, Qwen3.5-4B answering accuracy reaches 75%, versus 44% for the reranking baseline. Across 150 questions in three BRIGHT-derived domains, zero-shot average pass@1 reaches 52%, versus 20.8% for dense retrieval.

**Why it matters:** some repeated retrieval work can move into learned preparation before individual queries arrive.

**Limits:** construction consumes approximately 179,000 tokens, and the trained workspace increases answer-time reading. A synthetic-query reuse experiment improves tokens per expected correct answer, not every query's cost. The evaluation uses static corpora and modest test sets; changing documents, stale derived files, and larger collections remain open. This is not a production-memory validation.

### 3. Learning Self-Orchestration in Decentralized Agent Swarms

**October 15 | v1 preprint**  
[Metadata](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22322v1) · [Original paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.22322v1)

Specialized agents bid for each turn; the highest bidder acts. Bids also distribute downstream rewards backward. Action policies learn from this credit, while bidding policies learn through counterfactual rollouts that compare alternative turn allocations.

Three-agent teams are evaluated on Minecraft crafting, simulated navigation, and document research, against a monolithic reinforcement-learning baseline with matched training FLOPs. On 50 held-out research questions, accuracy is 21.0% ± 4.1% standard error versus 9.0% ± 2.1%, with three evaluations per question. Crafting and navigation rewards also improve.

**Why it matters:** coordination is learned together with competence rather than fixed solely by a prompt or workflow graph.

**Limits:** complementary roles are predefined, external rewards must be reliable, and experiments are small. Training bids requires restoring environment states for counterfactual alternatives; irreversible real-world effects do not offer that convenience. Larger training populations and agents above 10B parameters were not tested. These results do not imply that adding more conversational agents improves a team.

### 4. Improving Environment-Agnostic Coding Agents: The Role of Scaffolding

**October 15 | v1 preprint**  
[Metadata](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22289v1) · [Original paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.22289v1)

EnvTraverse compares a general Codex-based agent with a nearly identical variant instructed to report difficulties and return control for automatic continuation. Code, prompts, and general tools remain unchanged across ARC-AGI-3, AGI Maze, and Terminal-Bench; environment descriptions arrive as inputs.

On ARC-AGI-3, the instruction improves mean RHAE by about 1.4–7.3 points across four model/effort configurations. Scripted reset management recovers roughly one-third to three-fifths of the remaining gap to a specialized reference. Terminal-Bench changes are mixed: −1.50 to +2.25 points.

**Why it matters:** access to information and tools does not guarantee effective use of them. Small control policies can remain useful even with stronger models.

**Limits:** three retained evaluation blocks per configuration, three environment families, and no matched-inference-cost comparison. ARC gains accompany 2–52% more normalized token cost for the instruction variant; specialization costs more again. This does not isolate better organization from additional or differently allocated inference, and it does not establish universal benefit from the prompt change.

### 5. Approve What, Exactly? Effect Binding and Trusted Rendering for Agent Step-Up Authorization

**October 15 | v1 preprint**  
[Metadata](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21063v1) · [Original paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.21063v1)

ASU binds approval to a canonical effect descriptor, renders the approval from trusted fields, and executes the stored call rather than an agent's later resubmission. Its contribution is attack analysis and measurement around existing authorization ideas, not new cryptography.

The authors audit ten runtimes; four of eight measurable binding implementations permit post-approval substitution. In 27,108 scripted trials and 609 AgentDojo attack pairs, ASU blocks tested substitution, replay, and grant stretching. For 15 model approvers, trusted cards increase attack detection from 0.30 to 0.98, with a 0.16 false-deny rate largely driven by placeholder text.

**Why it matters:** the approved description and executed effect must remain connected. A visible approval button alone is insufficient evidence.

**Limits:** no human-subject data; small hand-built model-approver stimuli; scripted attacks rather than exhaustive adaptive adversaries. Formal guarantees assume trusted enrollment/rendering and appropriate decisions from trusted fields. A second device adds no measured advantage over trusted on-host confirmation in these experiments. Flooding, fatigue, and effects already allowed by policy remain unresolved.

## Reading takeaway

Preparation, coordination, control flow, and authorization are separate parts of an agent system. My engineering inference is to evaluate each at its own boundary: reusable knowledge must remain current, coordination must justify its cost, and permission must bind to the effect that actually occurs. None of these papers demonstrates that our proposed Salon checks have passed.
