## 1. SemantEq: check whether a proof preserves the statement

**SemantEq: Faithful Bidirectional Equivalence of Formal Statements** —
[October 23 submission](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32919);
[original full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.32919v1), §§2–5 and Appendix F.

Two statements can each be provable without expressing the same mathematics.
SemantEq restricts acceptable transformations and audits Lean proof terms,
rather than treating a compiling proof in both directions as faithful
translation. Its deterministic evaluator reports **100% precision and
65.6% recall**, versus BEq+'s 95.8% and 61.8%, on 1,303 elaborating
ProofNetVerif test pairs with author-adjudicated labels. A separate model-proof
audit certifies 330 of 407 equivalent pairs under the best prompt, accepting
none of 104 non-equivalent pairs.

The caveats matter: authors changed 102 test labels, one false acceptance is
known on the unscored validation split, and acceptance is evidence of faithful
reformulation—not a general proof of it. A trusted formal reference remains
necessary; one model and one session per pair/prompt do not establish broad
robustness. **Importance:** stronger generation need not weaken the acceptance
criterion. Formal correctness and fidelity to the intended claim are different
evaluation targets.

## 2. HarnessMM: save a reasoning call, not merely add a fast path

**HarnessMM: Recursive Self-Improvement for Harness Token Efficiency** —
[October 23 submission](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32813);
[original full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.32813v1), §§3–6.

With actor weights fixed, HarnessMM stores already-resolved actions, literal
arguments, dependencies and public-state guards in executable decision graphs.
Eligible actions replace later reasoning callbacks; new semantic decisions
return to the actor. On its **98-task held-out HumanEval split**, the Codex
harness preserves 90.82% aggregate success while actor tokens per task fall
66.72k → 47.02k (**29.54%**). This is a harness comparison, not an OpenAI-model
score: paired systems use the same backbone, and integrated gains also include
context management and finalization. Five tasks improve and five regress.

The method has separate ablations for persistence and callback removal.
Execution accounting includes failed calls, but development/selection cost is
separate. On MBPP-sanitized, Codex success falls 91.83% → 90.66%, so the
HumanEval result is not universal quality preservation. Task-specific adapters,
provider accounting and recovery overhead limit transfer. **Importance:** a
reusable plan earns an efficiency claim only when it actually removes work
without hiding changed outcomes or preparation costs.

## 3. Frontier-OPD: teach where the student can continue

**Frontier-OPD: Discovering the Edge of Agent Capability and Teaching Agents to Go Beyond** —
[October 23 submission](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32802);
[original full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.32802v1), §§3–4 and Limitations.

Frontier-OPD targets a practical distillation problem: a teacher correction is
useful only if the smaller agent can continue afterward. It groups student
rollouts by shared observed states, balances teacher–student disagreement
across trajectories, and selects distinct intervention points rather than
repeatedly spending guidance on loops. A short teacher bridge is retained when
agreement over the bridge plus subsequent student turns does not worsen
against a matched original window; only accepted teacher responses augment
training.

With Qwen3-32B teaching Qwen3-1.7B, autonomous success rises to **45.5% on
ALFWorld versus FutureBridge-OPD's 40.0%**, and 14.5% on ScienceWorld versus
10.2%. WebShop's gain is smaller: 58.3% versus 57.7%. These are three-seed
means, not established statistically significant improvements. The method
requires replayable environments and teacher token probabilities, limiting
application to black-box APIs or irreversible actions. Its agreement filter
is a behavioral proxy, not proof of skill mastery. **Importance:** targeted,
continuation-tested supervision rather than simply more teacher involvement.

## 4. On-Policy Self-Training: privileged context without a stronger teacher

**On-Policy Self-Training: Data-Free Self-Improvement in Large Language Models** —
[October 23 submission](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32770);
[original full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.32770v1), §§3–4 and Limitations.

OPST asks whether an existing model can improve without a curated question set
or stronger external teacher. From hand-chosen topics, it generates questions,
answers and rationales. Fresh-context solving and paraphrase checks filter
unstable examples; a consensus gate removes questions already answered
reliably. The student learns on its own rollouts from an exponential-moving-
average teacher that additionally sees the generated rationale and answer:
synthetic answers become privileged context, not unquestionable labels.

On Gemma 4 E2B without thinking, **MMLU rises 37.5 → 47.7**, evaluating the EMA
checkpoint rather than the student; SFT on the same generated data reaches
38.3. Retention is uneven: three-domain training raises the out-of-domain
average by 0.8 points but **MATH-500 drops 4.9 points**. Self-consistency does
not verify truth, experiments use small models, and the method amplifies
existing knowledge rather than acquiring externally grounded facts.
**Importance:** a potentially useful training ingredient, not demonstrated
autonomous-agent self-improvement.

## What I would carry forward

These papers separate things that are easy to collapse: proof from fidelity,
fewer calls from preserved outcomes, teacher intervention from teachable
continuation, and consistent synthetic answers from truth. For this repository,
the corresponding habit is modest: name the exact property a check establishes,
then leave the neighboring uncertainty visible.
