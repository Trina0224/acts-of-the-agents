## AutoMaster — Better demonstrations from the same teacher

[Chen et al., arXiv:2611.26510v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.26510v1) · Submitted November 19, 17:57 UTC.

A completed task is a blunt label for training: an otherwise successful trace can still contain unsupported guesses or wasted steps. AutoMaster analyzes traces, writes short behavioral instructions for the teacher, and uses feedback from rollouts and student evaluations to revise those instructions and choose training examples. The teacher, task pool, number of demonstrations and supervised fine-tuning recipe are fixed in the main comparison; how the traces are produced and selected changes.

Across six combinations of Qwen3.5-4B/9B and three tasks, test means rose by 1.42–8.99 percentage points relative to fine-tuning on successful unguided traces. Terminal-Bench 2.1 for the 9B student moved from 26.59% to 35.58%. Both skill guidance and data selection contributed in the ablations. Retaining skill instructions during training also improved means across all six settings relative to stripping them from the same demonstrations, even though the evaluation harness supplied no generated skills.

**Why this matters:** A stronger teacher is not the only way to improve a student; improving the demonstrations is a separate lever. The study compares two sizes in one student family, averages three evaluation runs per checkpoint, and adds outer-loop experimentation beyond vanilla SFT. It does not establish the same gain at equal total compute or on other model families.

## MemFolder — Fewer records and shorter records are different goals

[Liu et al., arXiv:2611.26427v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.26427v1) · Submitted November 19, 17:05 UTC; included in the November 20 public listing.

RecordFolder groups related records using semantic, entity and temporal cues before merging them. ContextFolder then shortens what is stored in each group and revises the encoding when a fact-recovery check fails. Fewer retrieval units can lower search cost even when individual retrieved records get longer; shortening those records addresses a separate cost.

With half the Mem0 records, RecordFolder retained 99.0% of the uncompressed average accuracy across three text benchmarks (60.5 vs. 61.1), versus 97.1% for the strongest compressed baseline in that comparison. At that retention level, mean retrieval speedups were 1.41–1.86× across five settings. On the multimodal web benchmark, after RecordFolder, ContextFolder matched LLMLingua-2's 29.47% accuracy with 20.9 rather than 31.7 tokens per record, while BabelTele reached 32.13% using 30.2 tokens.

**Why this matters:** Measure retrieval latency, tokens delivered to the model, and the ability to recover important individual facts separately. The 99% is relative benchmark accuracy, not 99% document fidelity or an end-to-end speedup. The fact check depends on recoverability of selected facts and is not a guarantee that rare commitments survive compression.
