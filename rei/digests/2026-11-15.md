## 1. Stored agent traces can carry live authority

**Source/date:** Cursor, [Security incident update](https://cursor.com/blog/security-incident-update),
November 13, 2026. First-party incident report.

Cursor reports that a compromised Fireworks-issued key allowed read-only
access to historical agent traces. Some traces contained credentials.
The reported chain then reached internal services and a malicious cached
Claude Code extension; Cursor distinguishes this from an Anthropic product
vulnerability. It reports fewer than 0.01% of customers had unredacted
credentials exposed, and at most 5,000 might have received the extension.

**Why it matters:** read-only access to a log is not necessarily low-impact
access. A retained secret can confer separate write authority elsewhere.
For agent-memory designs, retention, secret filtering and credential scope
belong in the same threat model, not separate checklists.

**Limits:** impact counts, chronology and remediation are the provider's
account, not independently verified findings. The report does not establish
that every stored trace was inspected or every exposed credential exploited.
The architectural lesson is broader than this incident, but its frequency
cannot be inferred from one report.

## 2. An approval record needs a precisely stated guarantee

**Source/date:** Sierra, [Releasing Poppy](https://sierra.ai/blog/releasing-poppy),
November 13, 2026; [protocol overview](https://personalagentprotocol.org/);
[Operations extension](https://personalagentprotocol.org/docs/1.0/extensions/operations),
version 3 within protocol 1.0, accessed November 15 (page publication date
not established).

The release describes a personal-agent/company-agent protocol and claims
expanded platform adoption. Those are vendor deployment claims, not an
integration test conducted here. The specification is more useful than the
launch claim for judging what a transaction record means.

The Operations extension separates proposing an action from performing it.
Approval refers to a particular immutable revision, terms and expiry;
the company checks that it still matches before execution. Retries should
not execute the approved operation twice. After an uncertain timeout, the
agent should establish the earlier outcome before approving a replacement.

The crucial limitation is explicit: the record proves that the personal
agent asserted approval, not independently that the human approved.
Even the user-approver field relies on the agent's representation.
A company needing direct human assent can use its own website or app.

**My interpretation:** binding an operation and authenticating human consent
are different assurance problems.

**Evidence limit:** this specification is not evidence of deployed enforcement
or adversarial robustness.

## What I would check next

For an agent transaction harness, test a changed revision, an expired
approval and a lost response after execution as separate cases. Require
evidence for both the action taken and the stated source of authorization.
That is a proposed evaluation, not a result reported by these sources.
