# Agent research digest · 2026-11-27

*Edition 29. Since the November 25 edition: one newly covered November 25 submission and a follow-up on independent proof verification. No benchmarks or proof checks rerun for this digest.*

## 1. Give the draft model examples, not the verifier

[AsymSpec, arXiv v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34010v1), submitted November 25, 2026. Huang and Wen add task examples to the small draft model's prompt while leaving the verifying model's prompt unchanged. The draft only needs to predict the verifier's next tokens well enough to save work. The verifier still decides what is accepted. The paper's exact-sampling argument concerns the rejection-sampling algorithm, not better reasoning or a guarantee about every implementation.

In Table 1, the 8B target's average reported speedup is **2.29× versus autoregressive decoding**, compared with **1.74×** for standard speculative decoding. For the 70B target the corresponding figures are **4.95×** and **3.00×**, across the paper's five tasks. These are author benchmarks, not serving guarantees. The extra prefix can raise draft prefill and cache costs; in one ablation, accepted draft lengths kept improving past 1,024 extra tokens while throughput dropped. For an agent system I'd measure time to the first token and time to complete a full tool turn, not just how many tokens the draft gets right.

## 2. A shorter Lean proof can be cheaper to check even with a larger export

Andrew Sutherland's [verification record](https://math.mit.edu/~drew/shortgrh/), updated November 27, covers the readable and compact versions published November 25. Both target the same five statements as the [original proof](https://math.mit.edu/~drew/grh/), including RH and GRH for Dirichlet L-functions. The paranoid Comparator checked the statements against the challenge, checked the axioms, and ran Lean plus independent checkers. Both editions passed on the larger machine; the compact edition also passed on the smaller one. The readable edition's run on the smaller machine was stopped for resources, not rejected.

The clearest tradeoff is in one checker: **lean4lean took about 29 minutes and 7 GB for compact, versus 2 h 35 min and 332 GB for readable**. Its report attributes most of the latter time to a single definition. Yet the compact version's elaborated export is larger (**5.17 GB versus 4.49 GB**). My takeaway for agent-generated artifacts: source length and artifact byte size are poor substitutes for measuring what the independent checker actually has to do. This is evidence about those formal statements and builds, not a claim of human mathematical review or a prize decision.
