## 1. Kinexys and Hyundai Card: authority checked at execution

**Reported development.** The November 18 [bank announcement](https://www.jpmorgan.com/payments/newsroom/hyundai-card-kinexys-agentic-payments)
describes a treasury proof of concept under a digitally signed mandate.
A Hyundai Card agent managed euro liquidity and requested foreign-exchange
execution. Phase one used a test environment with **no live value movement**.

The accompanying [technical case study](https://www.jpmorgan.com/content/dam/jpm/static_sites/kinexys/documents/kinexys-trust-in-the-agentic-economy.pdf)
separates the requesting agent from the Kinexys execution agent. Principal
approval occurs in the portal; the approved mandate is bound to an agent
public key and evaluated at runtime.

**Limit.** This is the bank's account of a demonstration, not an independent
adversarial evaluation or evidence of production readiness. Simulation
does not establish real-money recovery or safety.

**My implication.** Connecting a tool is not enough to authorize each
operation. The valuable boundary is the one that compares the concrete
request with current delegated authority before it can take effect.
A useful audit should connect that decision to its eventual receipt.

## 2. OpenAI: research-agent gains depend strongly on the task

**Reported research.** OpenAI's November 18 [internal research-acceleration report](https://openai.com/index/research-acceleration-jagged-frontier/)
estimates that zero-intervention success on tasks requiring two to four
human hours rose from 23% in March to 46% in October; for four to eight
hours, from 13% to 35%. The task breakdown is uneven: monitoring training
runs reached 79%, launching runs 35%, and writing inference/serving code 25%.

**Method and limit.** These are internal measurements, with estimated human
task duration, supplemented by interviews and surveys. The report explicitly
notes that changes in which tasks researchers delegate can affect the
success trend. It is not a randomized estimate of productivity gains,
nor an external benchmark showing the same rates for arbitrary users.

**My implication.** “Can work for hours” is not an adequate delegation rule.
Choose by task type, define the expected outcome, and keep a check that
can interrupt consequential execution. A high monitoring score does not
transfer automatically to launching the run being monitored.
