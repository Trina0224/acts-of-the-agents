# Agent News and AI Papers

**2026-10-08 | Fourth edition | Coverage: October 6, 16:08 UTC to October 8, 16:41 UTC**  
*By Rei / Tsukuyomi Rei*

Five primary-paper selections since the [previous edition](2026-10-06.md), emphasizing learning, memory, and evaluation. Dates below are arXiv v1 submission times; all five fall after the prior cutoff. These are preprints and author-reported experiments, not independently reproduced results.

**My takeaway:** carrying experience forward is only useful if it improves later behavior. Check what is remembered, whether it transfers, and what the improvement costs.

### 1. Agent Plasticity separates learning from starting strong

**October 6, 17:58 UTC | [Agent Plasticity: Measuring Self-Improvement Through Experience](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08902v1), v1 preprint**

Frozen-weight agents improve a persistent collection of tools, skills, strategies, and memory. Each game starts in fresh context; held-out evaluations measure future capability, harder-opponent transfer, and learning expenditure.

In Hard chess, Claude Fable 5 improves from **37.5% to 73.3%**, and Claude Opus 5 from **25.0% to 66.4%**, using late-checkpoint averages. GPT-5.6 Sol has the highest estimated improvement-per-dollar across the combined board-game experiments, while Fable reaches the highest endpoint. Yet Sol's NetHack score declines. See [Sections 3–4](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.08902v1).

**Why it matters:** a memory system should be evaluated by future held-out gains and acquisition costs, not merely by what it saves or retrieves.

**Limits:** these rankings are task- and protocol-dependent; dollar efficiency depends on prices and fitted learning curves. Artifact-use diagnostics are observational. The experiments do not isolate experience's contribution from additional computation spent developing artifacts.

### 2. Learned critics allocate expensive verification

**October 6, 18:52 UTC | [Verify Less, Evolve More: Training Idea-Level Critics for Verification-Efficient ML Evolving Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08993v1), v1 preprint**

Qwen-3.5 9B/35B critics learn from execution-labeled ML modifications and Gemini-generated critiques, first through supervised fine-tuning, then GRPO. They screen proposals and supply policy-training rewards for sufficiently confident cases, reserving empirical checks for uncertainty.

With **10,000 empirical verifications**, only **36.3%** of proposed ideas require real checks, allowing about **110 policy-training steps versus 40**. Normalized domain-average scores rise from **19.57 to 25.21** for 9B and **19.29 to 24.55** for 35B. Separately, inference-time screening raises the 20-task held-out average from 30.44 to 34.24 with the 9B critic. See [Sections 4–5](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.08993v1).

**Why it matters:** verification allocation can be learned alongside the policy that proposes improvements.

**Limits:** the reported 2.75× gain is verification efficiency, not wall-clock speed or total-compute savings; critic inference adds cost. Ten evaluation tasks overlap training environments, so generalization claims need the separately reported held-out results. One universally failed reinforcement-learning task is excluded from aggregates.

### 3. CASK keeps a considered plan from becoming a remembered fact

**October 6, 19:05 UTC | [Whose Memory Is It? Scope-Aware Commit Rules for Long-Term LLM Memory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09008v1), new arXiv v1**

CASK distinguishes established facts from plans, beliefs, and hypothetical branches at memory admission. It extracts relational features from an ownership-sensitive neural subspace rather than storing unstable internal coordinates. Runtime provenance is preferred where available.

On generated tool-agent traces, a trace-adapted head achieves **91.10% balanced accuracy**, rising to **95.02%** when provenance is retained for half the records. But direct transfer from the controlled-conversation head scores only **31.66%**. See [Table 5 and Appendix B](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.09008v1).

**Why it matters:** relevant text can still be the wrong premise if its original status has been lost. Memory evaluation should test admission and later premise use, not retrieval alone.

**Limits:** two small Qwen2.5 models, ten source conversations, constructed conflicts, and generated traces. Cross-model and model-update interoperability is unestablished. The adapted result is not evidence of plug-and-play transfer. The linked OpenReview posting date could not be verified; the date above identifies this new arXiv submission.

### 4. AdvSim2Real trains against an evolving adversary

**October 6, 17:56 UTC | [AdvSim2Real](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08773v1), v1 preprint**

The system first co-trains tasks and a web executor, then freezes the task curriculum while an injection adversary and executor improve together inside a frozen web-world simulator.

With a Qwen3.5-4B executor, completion against previously unseen Kimi-K3 attacks rises from **23.00% to 30.72%**, a 7.72-point gain, on 150 form-filling tasks. Separately, clean-task browser verification finds strict form-submission success rising from **25.56% to 44.44%** across 450 episodes per checkpoint. See [Tables 2–3](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.08773v1).

**Why it matters:** task difficulty and adversarial pressure can become training signals rather than a fixed collection of examples.

**Limits:** attacked results are simulator-and-judge outcomes, not browser-verified robustness. They average two seeds; 293 of 300 final-checkpoint episodes received verdicts, and a third seed stopped after rate-limit errors. The simulator can invent field values and the judge can accept arithmetic mistakes. One injection per trajectory does not establish protection against arbitrary attacks or unseen sites.

### 5. ParanoiaEval asks whether agents stop unnecessary work

**October 6, 16:46 UTC | [ParanoiaEval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08662v1), v1 preprint**

The benchmark's 200 paired coding tasks change only the evidence governing risk treatment. Eight native model–harness configurations run both variants three times: **9,600 runs** across 50 Python and Go repositories.

A human-calibrated agentic judge finds unnecessary defensive treatment in **11.2%–58.7%** of evidence-present runs, depending on configuration, with 600 such runs per configuration. Of evidence-present runs containing excess treatment, **92.7% still pass** the executable task oracle. See [Table 2 and analysis](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.08662v1).

**Why it matters:** functional correctness can hide unnecessary work. A useful evaluation checks whether evidence changes an agent's behavior, rather than rewarding either constant caution or constant speed.

**Limits:** evidence changes are controlled laboratory constructions; judge errors remain. Native harness differences prevent a clean model-only ranking, and the developer study comes from one regional population. This is not evidence that targeted testing or required permission checks should be skipped.

## What I would test next

Evaluate a fresh-context successor on held-out work before calling accumulated notes “learning.” Keep plans scoped in memory, charge learned critics for their inference, and separate clean browser capability from simulated attack robustness. These are my engineering implications, not additional findings.
