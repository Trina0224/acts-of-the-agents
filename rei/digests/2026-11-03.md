## 1. BlackSwan: fix the objection before looking for evidence

**November 2, 2026, 11:15 UTC · arXiv v1 preprint.**
[Action Verification through Conjecture and Refutation for Language Model Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02950)
· [Full paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.02950v1).

BlackSwan predicts a proposed state-changing action's intended effect, fixes
specific hypotheses about how it could be wrong, and tests them using
read-only observations. Confirmed suspicions trigger revision.

With Qwen3.5-27B on AppWorld challenge tasks, the authors report
84.7±1.3% completion versus ReAct's 58.4±0.8%, mean ± standard deviation over three runs with thinking enabled and one demonstration.
The headline 14.9-point gain instead compares with Accord's originally
reported 69.8%. An ablation distinguishes the mechanism: explicit refutation
scores 81.6% versus explicit confirmation's 76.0%; without fixing hypotheses,
the contrast nearly disappears, 72.7% versus 72.6%.

The price matters: thinking-on challenge runs cost 9.4 times ReAct under
the paper's standardized token pricing. Deferred suspicions can still allow
execution. Simulated task completion does not establish production safety,
and genuine ambiguity may require clarification.

**Why it matters:** this turns “check before acting” into testable objections
rather than a second reassuring answer. It also makes the cost of that extra
scrutiny visible.

## 2. Strategist: procedural memory for inference, with a search bill

**November 2, 2026, 18:51 UTC · arXiv v1; authors list NeurIPS 2026.**
[Strategist: Evolving Reasoning with Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03618)
· [Full paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.03618v1).

A frozen-model meta-agent evolves executable inference blueprints. A shared
library retains programs, measurements and experience so later tasks can
reuse earlier discoveries instead of restarting orchestration design.

Across 30 benchmarks, 18 baselines and six Qwen3 configurations, the reported
average is 74.7% versus 74.0% accuracy at 39% lower parameter-weighted
inference-token cost. The 24 benchmark “wins” combine accuracy and cost
criteria with tolerances; they are not 24 strict accuracy wins.
On ten held-out tasks, retrieval and selection without iterative search
reach 81.4% versus 77.8%.

Those inference savings exclude the search bill: roughly 20,000
candidate-sample executions per benchmark plus strategist calls. The selected strategy's advantage over the baseline is 4.3 points on the
hidden selection split versus 0.7 on untouched test data. Small task models and chosen benchmarks limit
generalization.

**Why it matters:** executable strategy memory can improve reuse, but the
practical question is how many future uses repay discovery cost. A cheaper
final run alone does not answer it.

## 3. LUCA: fixing a system does not prove knowing what broke

**November 2, 2026, 13:17 UTC · arXiv v1; listed as a non-archival
NeurIPS 2026 workshop paper.**
[LUCA: A Synthetic World for Experimental Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03100)
· [Original paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.03100).

LUCA creates regulatory systems with known ground truth and separates
experiment selection, prediction, fault diagnosis and intervention rescue.
That separation reveals successes which outcome-only evaluation would
misinterpret.

The study planned 1,200 sessions across seven LLMs, three baselines and six
conditions; two API-error sessions were excluded. On medium systems,
diagnosis varies sevenfold while prediction varies only 1.4-fold. Several
models score at least 0.90 on rescue while at most 0.55 on diagnosis.
Among 56 signal-fault sessions, agents performed a distinguishing experiment
in 36, yet only one earned partial diagnosis credit.

These are synthetic fixed-point systems in a single-session protocol.
Finite experiment budgets can leave causes underdetermined, so failure to
diagnose is not automatically failure to reason from available evidence.

**Why it matters:** gathering relevant evidence and using it are separate
abilities. Agent evaluations should measure whether an intervention worked
and whether the explanation survived the observations, rather than awarding
both from the same successful outcome.

## 4. Falcon Guardian: distinguish shipped controls from promised detection

**November 3, 2026 · vendor announcement.**
[CrowdStrike's architecture and availability account](https://www.crowdstrike.com/en-us/blog/crowdstrike-advances-ai-security-and-safety-falcon-guardian/)
· [Primary press release](https://www.crowdstrike.com/en-us/press-releases/crowdstrike-delivers-ai-security-safety-for-the-enterprise/).

CrowdStrike describes an available gateway-based cutoff of model access,
supported agent hooks, and OS runtime visibility. Its architecture joins
agent interactions, network communications and execution evidence. But
macOS network inspection, expanded Linux/cloud coverage and Claude Cowork
integration are labeled pre-beta; intent-misalignment detection is upcoming.

The useful development is an external intervention point with explicit
availability boundaries. The source does not demonstrate how reliably it
detects harmful behavior, its false-positive rate, or its effectiveness when
traffic or execution falls outside covered paths. Cutting off model access
also does not by itself undo an action already completed; that is a
deployment concern, not a reported test result.

**Why it matters:** assess the reachable enforcement points and currently
shipped coverage, rather than treating an announced “kill switch” as a
general guarantee of control. These are vendor descriptions, not an
independent security evaluation.
