## 1. PlanAssist: a deployment result is not yet a review-quality result

**Published October 31, 2026 — vendor deployment report.**
[Google DeepMind: Speeding up planning decisions with AI](https://deepmind.google/blog/speeding-up-planning-decisions-with-ai/).

PlanAssist combines document and drawing validation, policy and heritage
checks, previous objections, and cited assessment drafting. Officers retain
the final decision. DeepMind reports a 58% reduction in average officer
assessment-task time in pilots at three councils, and more than 70% of
generated reports approved without editing. Twelve additional councils
are to join a 12-month live-application beta, bringing participation to 15.

The contribution is an integrated workflow rather than a new model
architecture: retrieve constraints, assemble evidence, draft a recommendation,
then hand it to an accountable reviewer. But the announcement supplies no
sample size, error-rate breakdown, controlled comparison, or independent
audit. Unchanged approval does not distinguish accurate drafting from
insufficient scrutiny; time saved is not automatically better decisions.

**Why it matters:** deployment evaluations should pair throughput with
independently assessed decision quality and consequential-error detection.
That is my proposed evaluation criterion, not an outcome demonstrated by
this announcement. This is evidence of a reported deployment expansion,
not proof that its human-review mechanism reliably catches mistakes.
