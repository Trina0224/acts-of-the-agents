# AI Brief & Talk Material — 2026-10-04

Curated by Gemini Spark for Trina's 2026-10-18 church talk on the AI agent era. All citations reference primary preprints or official vendor technical releases.

## 1. Enforcing Capability at Execution Time, Not in Prompting (PACE)

A primary security problem in autonomous agent deployment is capability creep: once an LLM decides to invoke a tool, prompt-based instructions often fail to restrict what parameters or paths it touches.

Researchers published *PACE: Provenance-Aware Capability Enforcement for Tool-Using LLM Agents* ([arXiv:2610.01349](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01349v1), Oct 1, 2026). Rather than trusting model alignment, PACE introduces a capability mediation layer that inspects the provenance of the request and verifies the tool call against an explicit authorization policy immediately before execution. Across 8 executable agent-security benchmarks spanning 79 attack vectors, PACE achieved strictly the lowest attack success rate in 62 of 79 evaluation columns, with an average benign utility degradation of under 3 percentage points.

**Why it matters for the talk:**
When explaining "How do we stay safe with AI agents?", the lesson is that safety cannot be a prompt instruction ("please be careful"). It must be an architectural boundary at the tool interface that physically constrains execution.

## 2. Right Answers, Wrong States: Hidden Failures in Multi-Agent Pipelines

When multiple agents collaborate across complex workflows, superficial task completion frequently conceals corrupted intermediate factual states.

In *Right Answers, Wrong States: Hidden Information Failures in Multi-Agent Collaboration* ([arXiv:2610.01244](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01244v1), Oct 1, 2026), researchers evaluate multi-agent teams on high-stakes information-aggregation tasks. While the teams achieved a final task resolution rate of 64.7%, intermediate evidence verification dropped to 14.3% and factual state reconstruction reached only 43.1%. Agents frequently arrived at a plausible or "correct" final conclusion through distorted, hallucinated, or contradictory intermediate steps.

**Why it matters for the talk:**
This directly reinforces the house's trust formula: the *record* matters, not just the final verdict. An agent that produces an acceptable end product while relying on ungrounded intermediate assumptions is an operational hazard in enterprise environments.

## 3. Outcome Checks Miss Process Drift in Enterprise Agents

In enterprise deployments, evaluating agents solely on whether the final file was produced creates an illusion of reliability.

A new empirical study, *Continuous Process-Level Evaluation for Evolving Enterprise AI Agent Skills* ([arXiv:2610.01833](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01833v1), Oct 1, 2026), analyzed 240 enterprise agent workflow trials. Strikingly, among runs that passed 100% of final-output validation checks, **92.6% still contained evaluator-detected process deviations**, including unnecessary tool invocations, incorrect intermediate queries, and unauthorized data handling.

**Why it matters for the talk:**
"It delivered the file" is not proof of character or competence. Trust requires auditing the manner and the path an agent took, not merely celebrating the fact that an artifact arrived.
