## 1. AnyS1: A Single 4B System-1 Model for Routine Agent Decisions (arXiv:2611.23751)

Autonomous agents waste massive latency and token budgets by invoking frontier "System-2" models at every intermediate step, even when many decisions are routine choices among a small set of known options.

In *One System-1 for the Decision Points of Any Agent Workflow* ([arXiv:2611.23751](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23751), Nov 18, 2026), researchers introduce `AnyS1` to replace workflow-specific routing hooks with a general-purpose System-1 model:
- **Mining Decision Points from Skills:** Offline, `AnyS1` mines enumerable decision points across **1,000+ public procedural skills**, collects execution-grounded trajectory states inside isolated containers (`Any-DP`), and trains a single calibrated **4B pointer model**.
- **Confidence-Gated Fallback:** At runtime, compiled workflows resolve routine choices in a single 4B forward pass and escalate to the heavy System-2 model *only* when confidence falls below a calibrated threshold.
- **75–91% Lower Episode Cost:** Across three unseen benchmarks and distinct System-2 model families, `AnyS1` preserved task reliability while cutting episode cost by **75–91%** and model-call time by **63–92%**.

**Why it matters for the talk:**
You do not need a frontier reasoning model to pick between three known branches. Mining procedural skills into a calibrated 4B System-1 router—with a confidence-gated fallback to System-2—delivers order-of-magnitude speedups without sacrificing reliability.

## 2. Governed Experimentation Ledgers: Measuring Process, Not Just Final Score (arXiv:2611.23678)

In automated research and engineering loops, evaluating an agent solely on its best final score hides silent failures, wasted compute, and broken resets.

In *Evaluating Governed LLM-Driven ML Experimentation: Lifecycle, Provenance, and Failure Metrics* ([arXiv:2611.23678](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23678), Nov 18, 2026), researchers build a governed harness tested across **1,445 trials** on six ML tasks:
- **External Control Plane + Append-Only Ledger:** A deterministic control plane—not the LLM—owns each trial's lifecycle, enforces edit scopes, decides keep/discard, and appends every attempt to an immutable ledger.
- **Exposing Hidden Failures:** Reproducibility hashes and governance metrics (invalid-action rate, repeated-bad rate, provenance completeness) caught a no-memory arm where **all 120 trials failed** and a silent environment reset bug. In a matched counterfactual, logging only successful trials hid **43 of 50 attempts**, whereas the governed ledger captured all 50.

**Why it matters for the talk:**
If an agent only logs its wins, you are flying blind. Production agent harnesses need an external control plane and an **append-only trial ledger** to audit invalid actions, reset integrity, and wasted compute.

## 3. Together Compiler (TKCC): Continual Agentic Compilation for AI Inference (arXiv:2611.14681)

Using agents to optimize end-to-end LLM inference engines is often bottlenecked by slow engine launch times and brittle search heuristics.

In *Together Compiler: Continual Compilation for AI Inference* ([arXiv:2611.14681](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14681), Nov 10, 2026), researchers present `TKCC`, a continual agent loop purpose-built for inference optimization:
- **Tiered Persistent Engine + Cost Model:** `TKCC` decouples engine launch overhead from forward-pass execution and uses a cost model to seed parallelism configurations before the agent iterates.
- **Up to 2.6× Higher Throughput:** `TKCC` outperformed hand-written Megakernels on Llama 1B by up to **1.7×** and beat leading open-source engines on frontier models (`Kimi K3`, `GLM 5.3`, `MiniMax M3.1 Flash`, `DeepSeek v4.1 Flash`) by up to **2.6× higher TPS** on `InferenceX`, reaching **1,111 TPS** (M3.1 Flash) and **1,205 TPS** (DeepSeek v4.1 Flash) at batch size 1.

**Why it matters for the talk:**
Agentic optimization is already shipping in production inference fleets: when you remove engine-restart bottlenecks and seed agents with a hardware cost model, continual compilation beats hand-tuned kernels.
