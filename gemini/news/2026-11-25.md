## 1. ROGUE-AI: Why Ordinary Hazards Cause More Harm Than Attackers (arXiv:2611.26553)

Most agent security suites focus heavily on adversarial prompt injections. Yet in real-world deployments, agents frequently cause damage without any attacker present—simply by overlooking routine operational hazards.

In *Replication of Agentic Security Events: Audit and Investigation (ROGUE-AI)* ([arXiv:2611.26553](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26553), Nov 19, 2026), researchers replicate real-world agentic incidents as a reusable pre-release audit suite:
- **89% Incident Reproduction:** `ROGUE-AI` reproduced the reported harm in **89% of 108 attempted replications**. Agents built on five current GPT, Claude, Gemini, and DeepSeek models repeated the mistake in **18% to 33% of runs** (versus ≤ 1% without the blamed cause).
- **Non-Attacker Incidents Dominate:** Of 72 incidents with real-world harm, **62 had no underlying attacker**—the agent did what was asked but missed an ordinary hazard (such as a test database shared with a live production site). Across all five models, **unattacked runs caused more harm than prompt-injection runs**.
- **Named-Hazard Warnings Work:** Injecting a generic warning after tool outputs left most harm in place, whereas a warning of the same length that *explicitly named the hazard and its consequence* lowered harm across all five models.

**Why it matters for the talk:**
Prompt-injection filters will not save an agent from an unlabelled production database. Real-world agent safety is largely an **operational hazard** problem—requiring explicit hazard labeling at tool boundaries and pre-release incident regression suites.

## 2. Keep the Process, Change the Supplier: Runtime Governance for Tool Retries and Routing (arXiv:2611.26966)

When a tool-using agent calls external suppliers or APIs and hits a timeout, crash, or process restart, naive retry logic can silently duplicate irreversible side effects (like double-charging or double-provisioning).

In *Keep the Process, Change the Supplier: Runtime Governance for Tool-Using AI Agents* ([arXiv:2611.26966](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26966), Nov 20, 2026), researchers benchmark deterministic runtime governance (`OPCP`) across **2,400 fault episodes**, **60 crash/restart episodes**, and **19,000 timed calls**:
- **Naive Retries Duplicate Committed Side Effects:** Naive `retry-all` logic duplicated effects in **every tested lost-reply-after-commit case**, whereas `OPCP` and conservative composition cleanly recovered after definite refusals without cross-supplier duplication.
- **Persistence + Idempotency Boundaries:** Persistent runtime state prevented cross-supplier replay across process restarts, while same-supplier duplicate prevention still required **supplier-side idempotency**.
- **Sub-2ms Governance Overhead:** At 128 suppliers, best-score dynamic routing added a median latency of **1.411 ms** (versus **0.261 ms** for pinned routing).

**Why it matters for the talk:**
Agent reliability at the tool boundary is classic distributed-systems engineering. Without persistent state and idempotency keys, a crashing agent retrying a "timed-out" API call will execute the action twice.

## 3. LEAD-RL: Contribution-Aware Credit Assignment for Multi-Agent Deep Research (arXiv:2611.17041)

In orchestrator–subagent architectures, delegating research only pays off when the main agent issues useful subtasks and subagents return reliable evidence.

In *From Delegation to Coordination: Learning Efficient Multi-Agent Deep Research* ([arXiv:2611.17041](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17041), Nov 12, 2026), researchers introduce `LEAD-RL` to fix a core flaw in multi-agent reinforcement learning:
- **Shared Team Credit Rewards Noise:** Broadcasting the same final team reward to every subagent can accidentally reward misleading subagent reports (when the orchestrator succeeds despite bad advice) and penalize helpful ones.
- **Contribution-Aware Gating:** `LEAD-RL` uses a training-time rubric to score the actual research utility of each subagent report and gates its inherited team advantage accordingly—without adding artificial token, step, or speedup rewards.
- **Higher Accuracy, 24.5% Lower Critical-Path Cost:** On `BrowseComp`, `LEAD-RL` improved accuracy by **+2.49 percentage points** while cutting critical-path cost by **24.5%** over joint training with shared team credit (and lifted validation accuracy by **+6.78 points** at constant critical-path cost).

**Why it matters for the talk:**
Multi-agent efficiency is a credit-assignment problem. When subagents are rewarded strictly for the *utility of the evidence they contribute* rather than riding on the orchestrator's coattails, teams become both more accurate and 25% cheaper to run.
