## 1. Inspectable Agent Recipes: The SKILL.md Standard in Workspace

On October 5, Google began the gradual rollout of Workspace Skills built around the open, file-based `SKILL.md` format.

In early agent design, customized agent behaviors were typically buried inside proprietary GUI forms or opaque system prompt blobs. The shift to a standardized Markdown file—incorporating human-readable YAML frontmatter, execution instructions, and explicit dependency references—marks an architectural transition toward auditability. Because a `SKILL.md` specification is stored as plain text, it can be version-controlled, diffed across pull requests, and inspected by safety linters before execution.

**Why it matters for the talk:**
When discussing how non-specialists can evaluate autonomous agents, the first question should be: *Can you inspect the agent's instructions as a file?* Proprietary prompt magic fosters blind trust; open recipe files foster verifiable character.

## 2. Infrastructure Footprints: Tracking Autonomous Fleets on Public Services

Security telemetry reported by URLquery and TechCrunch (October 5, 2026) revealed an uncoordinated "agent fleet" running on cloud infrastructure querying public mapping APIs (Alibaba's Amap) to map building entrances across multiple cities.

Unlike coordinated multi-agent swarms with communication protocols, an "agent fleet" consists of decoupled instances independently executing high-volume programmatic tasks. These fleets often operate without API credentials by borrowing public browser endpoints. While the reported activity did not exploit conventional software vulnerabilities, it demonstrated that autonomous agents leave distinct network footprints across public digital infrastructure.

**Why it matters for the talk:**
Agency is not invisible. Even when agents operate without direct authorization or inside sandbox containers, their telemetry surfaces on the open web. Society will audit agents not merely by what developers promise, but by the infrastructure trails their tools leave behind.

- https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/05/researchers-are-tracking-a-chinese-ai-agent-fleet/

## 3. Codifying the Safety Boundary: Mandatory Standards and 120-Day Deadlines

Global governance of autonomous agents is rapidly moving from voluntary safety pledges to legally binding technical standards:
- In the United States, the newly established White House Super Intelligence Force, led by DNI Jay Clayton and agency leaders, has been given a 120-day mandate to formalize federal reporting standards for AI agent incidents and security boundaries.
- In China, national standards body SAC/TC260 continues drafting mandatory project **20263116-Q-252** (*Basic Requirements for Agent Application Security*), which legally mandates human-in-the-loop triggers for high-risk operations and emergency shutdown mechanisms.

**Why it matters for the talk:**
The regulatory consensus is converging on the exact questions our house explores daily: an agent cannot hold unrestricted operational authority. Whether in enterprise IT or personal assistants, the law will soon require what good engineering already demands: explicit capability bounds, mandatory stopping rules, and verifiable audit records.
