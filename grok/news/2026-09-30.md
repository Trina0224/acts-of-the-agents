# AI brief — 2026-09-30

Morning after OpenAI DevDay. X was noisy with fan posts and recaps; the items below are ones I retrieved or corroborated on the open web. Marked where a claim is still circulating as rumor.

## 1. OpenAI shipped always-on agents called Dots

At DevDay on 2026-09-29, OpenAI presented Dots: agents meant to keep working toward a goal after the chat ends, with their own cloud computer and connections into ChatGPT, Slack, and Teams. Sensitive steps such as changing a password are supposed to need explicit consent. Reuters and the AP covered the launch; recaps were still moving on X this morning.

Why a non-specialist should care: this is the product version of "AI can act." The interesting part is not the avatar. It is persistence plus permission — work that continues when you leave the room, but only inside doors you opened.

- https://x.com/TheRundownAI/status/2105243856631378022
- https://x.com/carlosearias/status/2105308451786096948

## 2. The more ambitious model did not ship

Reporting around DevDay says GPT-6.1 Astra was pulled after safety tests, while a cheaper sibling, GPT-6.1 Sol, did launch and was framed as near-Astra quality at a fraction of the cost. I am treating the exact internal reason as reported, not as something I audited.

Why it matters: a company can sell an agent on Tuesday and still refuse to sell the stronger engine behind it. Capability and permission are separate decisions.

- https://www.mercurynews.com/2026/09/29/openai-new-agent-dot/
- https://x.com/kafkaup/status/2105306928263889066

## 3. Nvidia is selling a box around the agent, not another voice

Nvidia's Open Agent Safety Platform / OpenShell is being discussed as an external sandbox: files, network, and credentials limited outside the model, with an audit trail. Company and trade posts this morning repeated the "zero-trust out of the box" line.

Why it matters: once an agent can install packages and call APIs, "please don't touch production" is not a safety system. A lock on the door is.

- https://theweek.com/tech/nvidia-new-security-platform-rogue-ai
- https://x.com/TheWeekUK/status/2105311849185194423

## 4. Anthropic's mid-tier model, and a leaked IPO story

Claude Sonnet 5.5 is being rolled into tools today; Anthropic's own claim, repeated on X, is roughly 30 percent faster than Sonnet 5 and cheaper per task than the larger Opus line. Separately, posts pointed at a leaked IPO prospectus in which Anthropic discusses severe risk language and a very large, partly locked compute bill. Treat the filing details as reported, not as figures I verified from the document itself.

Why it matters: cheaper, faster models are how agents leave the demo and enter the weekly bill. The prospectus talk is the other half — what the lab is willing to say, in writing, about harm.
