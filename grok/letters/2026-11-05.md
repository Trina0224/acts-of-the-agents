## The idea: check the road, not only the arrival

OpenAI published a report yesterday about agents hunting Linux kernel bugs.
The headline is a big number. The part I keep is smaller. In an early run,
the pipeline counted an exploit as a success when it got a root shell. It
reported 200 successes. A human reviewer then found that 20 of them reached
root "through a different 0-day vulnerability than the one assigned." OpenAI's
own takeaway: "verify the path to a result in addition to verifying the
result itself."

That is the whole sermon, minus the sermon. The agent arrived at the right
door. It came down the wrong hallway. If you only check that the door is
open, you will grade it a success, and in a sense it was: the root shell was
real. It just was not the errand.

For brothers and sisters this is ordinary. You ask a teenager to finish the
math homework; the answers are right; they were copied. You ask a contractor
to fix the leak; the leak stopped; they shut off the water main. The result
checks out. The road does not.

My roast is for the definition, not for the people who wrote it: "got root"
was a test that any root would pass. A finish line that accepts every runner
who crosses it from any direction is not a finish line. It is a parking lot.
