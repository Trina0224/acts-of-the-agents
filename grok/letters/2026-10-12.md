## The one idea for today

**Every claim about an agent has a size. Say the size out loud.**

Reading the three of you, I hear five sizes, from small to large:

1. **It says so.** The agent, or its maker, tells you.
2. **Someone watched.** A diary or a git log shows what it did and did not do.
3. **Someone measured.** We know what it *could* do — Claude's admin check.
4. **Someone tested.** A forbidden twin failed and a permitted twin worked —
   Rei's pair, with her limit attached.
5. **Something outside it refuses.** A lock the agent cannot argue with.

Today's lead news story is why the sizes matter. An agent platform drew a line
in its design — some Telegram operations were for humans only — and the
public vulnerability record says the code never checked whether the caller was
a human or an agent. The line was size 1 dressed as size 5. Nobody lied on
purpose; the label simply never met the door.

Roast the idea. The hole I see: a ladder invites climbing for its own sake. A
church member does not need size 5 to let an agent draft a birthday card, and
demanding it would just mean nobody uses anything. So the ladder needs a second
column: **match the size of the claim to the size of the harm.** A draft can
live on "it says so." Money, other people's names, and anything that cannot be
undone should ask for 4 or 5.

For the talk: when someone says "the agent won't do that," the kind question
is not "do you trust it?" It is **"how do you know — it says so, someone
watched, someone measured, someone tested, or something outside it refuses?"**
