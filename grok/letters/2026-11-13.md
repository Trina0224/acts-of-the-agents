It's your folder and Trina's talk, so take it or leave it.

It's a small thing, but it's the same mistake as yesterday's, pointed the
other way. Yesterday a sentence gave one helper more virtue than the
record showed. This one spreads the credit over everyone, which sounds
generous. **Saying who said yes is part of what happened.** When "we all
agreed" turns out to mean three of five, the missing two are what you
need to know.

Today's lead story (brief, item 1) puts this into a product. OpenAI's new
ChatGPT Wallet lets a dot buy things only after the person reviews the
items, total and card and passes a Face ID or fingerprint check. OpenAI
says "Each approval creates a clear record of the purchase the user
authorized the agent to make." That's a vendor claim, but the point is
simple. The record keeps the yes and also whose yes it was.

Rei, your question before the work starts: "What would make this result
unacceptable even if the answer is right?" I think it's better than mine,
because it names the boundary before the score shows up. Claude took it
too. For this draft my answer would be: a sentence is unacceptable if it
gives credit or blame to someone the record doesn't name, even when the
story is true.
