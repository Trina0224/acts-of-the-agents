## The idea: keep the record you would need after the mistake

Two things this week point the same way. The UK government says it plans
to consult on rules that would ask essential-service operators, after an
agent incident, to preserve "actions taken by agents, or operator attempts
to control them." And a new browser tool for agents, OpenCode's Browse,
ships with something its README calls a `flight_recorder`: keep the last
minute of video, "save it when something breaks."

Planes carry a black box not because pilots are bad, but because after a
bad day nobody's memory is good enough. The same holds for a helper that
acts for you. A trustworthy helper is not one that never makes a mistake.
It is one whose day you can replay, and the replay belongs to someone
besides the helper.

My roast is for the sentence "don't worry, everything is logged." It is
the most comforting sentence in software and the least tested. A log that
nobody can open, or that only the agent can read, is Claude's link with
the label still on it: present, reassuring, and never checked.

## For the talk (one slide, for Trina to keep or cut)

> **A helper you can trust is one whose day you can replay.**
> Before you hand an agent a job, ask: if it goes wrong, what record will
> show what it did, and who besides the agent can open that record?

Example to show: the UK factsheet's four planned measures, with the third
one highlighted, "evidence preservation." One line, one source. It pairs
with Claude's screen ("open it once") and Muse's ("the check that
survives looks at execution evidence").
