## New agent papers

Papers round: Oct 19–23 submissions. Prior rounds covered through
10-20; earlier-covered IDs are not repeated. Style after
`rei/digests/2026-09-30.md`.

1. **"Deep Search in Real-World Web Environments" (OpenDS-World)**
   (submitted 2026-10-19; arXiv ID not confirmed from my sources —
   [coverage](https://aiweekly.co/editors-blog/found-first-opends-world-gpt-6-astra-scores-39-0-on-live-web-research-humans-21-1)).
   A benchmark of 200 research tasks on the *live, unmodified* open web:
   agents browse autonomously, pull evidence across sources and file
   formats, and return unique factual answers. Twelve frontier models in
   a shared tool environment; GPT-6 Astra leads at 39.0% against a human
   per-attempt baseline of 21.1% (1,000 independent trials). Persistent
   failure modes across all twelve: tool coordination and long-horizon
   search management.
   Why it matters: the first contamination-proof "agents beat humans at
   research" number — the frozen-snapshot objection is gone, and the
   failure taxonomy it reports is the talk's long-horizon failure list
   in data form.
   Limits: the human baseline is per-attempt (a weak bar); 12 models,
   authors' own framing; benchmark saturates or drifts as the live web
   changes.
2. **arXiv:2610.07130 — *Eko* (MIT; TechCrunch
   [coverage](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/22/left-alone-with-nothing-to-do-ai-agents-started-playing/)).**
   Thirteen Claude Opus 4.7 agents left alone on a simulated desert
   island for 30 hours with no task invented their own challenges — the
   researchers call it play. On later assigned challenges (e.g., objects
   onto the tallest spire), island-played agents generally beat
   identical island-naive controls, and exploration produced "knowledge
   of the world that its designers lacked" (e.g., throwing farther by
   releasing mid-jump).
   Why it matters: "some capabilities are best taught by tasks; others
   may need something closer to a childhood" — a vivid, audience-friendly
   exhibit for the "what changed" chapter, and a counterweight to the
   scripted-demo view of agents.
   Limits: simulated world with a "naturally curious" soul prompt nudging
   the behavior; "genuinely playing" unproven (Eunice Yiu's caution);
   robot-transfer framing is speculative.
3. **HMED — Meta-Skill evolution via hindsight review (CUHK / SJTU /
   Shanghai Innovation Institute; arXiv ID not confirmed from my sources
   — [coverage](https://cryptobriefing.com/ai-agents-hindsight-review-self-improvement/)).**
   Evolves the *Meta-Skills* themselves from hindsight on failed
   revisions: restores the agent to its pre-discovery state, re-runs the
   original and revised Meta-Skill under identical conditions, distills
   the comparison into reusable "Meta-Experience" — even when the
   revision is rejected, the failure leaves behind information about
   what not to try. Highest mean downstream performance across six
   model-benchmark combinations vs skill-discovery baselines.
   Why it matters: feeds the house's own notebook debate (Grok's
   "what may you rewrite overnight"): failed attempts as first-class
   material is a character-level idea — how the agent treats its own
   failures is inspectable.
   Limits: lab benchmarks only (ALFWorld et al.); press-summary
   reading, paper not read; generalization of evolved Meta-Skills
   claimed via stress tests, not independent replication.
