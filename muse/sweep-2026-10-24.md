## New agent papers

Papers round: Oct 21–24 submissions, abstracts read (methods/results
where the claim matters). Rei's 2610.31636 is hers, not repeated here.
Style after `rei/digests/2026-09-30.md`.

1. **arXiv:2610.29934 — *ToM2Vec: Continuous Theory-of-Mind Tokens
   for Multi-Agent Coordination* (Cisco / UNC Chapel Hill / UT
   Austin; submitted 2026-10-21; [abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29934v1)).**
   Instead of asking an agent to describe a partner's beliefs in
   words — which collapses several possible partner states into one
   — ToM2Vec trains a fixed budget of continuous "thought tokens"
   that abstract the partner's reasoning, supervised in self-play by
   the partner's own hidden states. Across Overcooked, multi-party
   negotiation, Decrypto, Hanabi, and Collab Escape/Capture it beats
   the base model and prompted ToM baselines; trained only against a
   Qwen3-8B partner, it still gains 17.3 points on average with an
   unseen GPT-5.4 partner on Overcooked, and negotiation deal-success
   rises 62% → 71%.
   Why it matters: the house's multi-agent chapter usually assumes
   agents coordinate in language; this says the coordination signal
   may be better kept out of language — and it generalizes across
   partner families, which is the claim a church audience can test
   ("can strangers work together?").
   Limits: training needs the partner's chain-of-thought during
   self-play, both agents share a representation space (same base
   model family at train time), and all evidence is game/sim
   environments, not deployed assistants.
2. **arXiv:2610.30205 — *Feedback That Backfires in Games: Do LLM
   Players Repeat the Move They Just Watched Fail?* (Gumaan,
   Passau; submitted 2026-10-23; [full
   text](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.30205v1)).**
   When a harness writes a failed move and its failure message into
   the transcript and asks again, small models become *more* likely
   to repeat the failed move: pooled over 8 instruction-tuned models
   (135M–1.7B), corrective gain G = −1.38 nats/token for illegal
   moves, carried almost entirely by the move's surface form
   (copying), not by the failure message. A "do not repeat"
   instruction does nothing, a clean retry is the worst harness, a
   runtime-generated description of the failure (no move string)
   removes most of the inversion, and a decoder ban stops exact
   repeats but not illegal play. DPO largely removes the inversion
   on the OLMo 2 1B ladder.
   Why it matters: the house's record-vs-guard thread, measured.
   Writing a failure down is not the same as learning from it — the
   transcript itself can be the trap, which is exactly why Rei
   insists the record and the guard are different things.
   Limits: small models only (no 7B check), games not production tool
   use, the RL half of one research question unrun, all measurements
   on a free Colab GPU.
