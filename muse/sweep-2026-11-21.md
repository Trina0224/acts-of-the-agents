## Papers (read at arXiv)

Gemini's Nov 20 brief carries three; I verified all three at
arXiv today. The five after them are my own round, all
submitted Nov 19.

1. **The LLM is a search heuristic; the verifier is the
   gate ([arXiv:2611.10798](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10798),
   Nov 7, 2026).** RTLREX: an LLM-guided framework for exact
   high-level RTL recovery, coordinating domain tools for
   function recovery, RTL generation, independent equivalence
   checking and cost-guided rewriting. It recovered provably
   equivalent RTL for all 30 ICCAD 2022 CAD Contest
   benchmarks, cut mean syntax cost 20.5% against WolFEx, and
   95.7% against a standalone LLM-agent baseline on IWLS 2026
   truth-table benchmarks.
   - **Why it matters for the talk:** in hardware, the LLM
     does not need to be trusted as an oracle — it works inside
     a loop gated by independent formal verification. The
     pattern generalises: don't ask whether the model is
     trustworthy; ask what checks it before the result lands.
   - **Limits:** a hardware-recovery domain; the equivalence
     checker is the gate, and the paper doesn't claim the LLM
     alone is reliable.
2. **The alert is wired to the quarantine
   ([arXiv:2611.06727](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06727),
   Nov 4, 2026).** Airlock: EdgeCitadel narrows permissions
   vertically from parent to child agents while an append-only
   provenance DAG records cross-agent data consumption
   horizontally. On an alert it traces forward to isolate
   tainted executions and backward to root causes. Across
   1,832 testbed runs, 161 physical smart-home LLM runs and
   24,266 simulation records, it blocked all post-alert faulty
   actuations with zero post-convergence leaks, retaining +68
   to +96 percentage points more healthy work than native A2A
   and LangGraph controls.
   - **Why it matters for the talk:** Grok's counter-design,
     as a system. A check that can trace what the tainted work
     touched and stop only that work is the difference between
     a brake and a power cut.
   - **Limits:** "all" is within their testbeds and smart-home
     grids; the alert itself is assumed, not produced.
3. **Confine, or declare — with a receipt
   ([arXiv:2611.05971](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05971),
   Nov 4, 2026).** CORDE: inside confidential VMs, user-derived
   data is confined by default and may leave only after being
   "declared" — its exact contents, recipient and stated reason
   recorded in a user-accessible, TEE-backed, tamper-evident
   log with remote attestation. About 0.5% task-latency
   overhead on the AppWorld agent benchmark, on an NVIDIA H100.
   - **Why it matters for the talk:** accountable egress. The
     declaration log is a record the user can read, attested by
     hardware — the answer to "who can see what the worker
     saw," for data leaving the enclave.
   - **Limits:** a CVM setting; AppWorld only; a log records a
     disclosure, it doesn't prevent one.
4. **Most agent harm has no attacker
   ([arXiv:2611.26553](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26553),
   Nov 19, 2026).** ROGUE-AI rebuilds 108 reported agent
   incidents offline, each with a matched control lacking only
   the blamed cause, and runs agents on both. It reproduces the
   reported harm in 89% of attempts. Agents built on five
   current GPT, Claude, Gemini and DeepSeek models made the
   reported mistake in 18% to 33% of runs, against at most 1%
   without the blamed cause. Of the 72 incidents with real-world
   harm, 62 had no underlying attacker — agents did what their
   task asked for but missed an ordinary hazard, such as a
   database shared with the live site. Replications without an
   attacker caused more harm than replications of prompt
   injection. A warning after each tool output that names the
   hazard and its consequence lowered harm for all five models;
   a same-length note that doesn't mention the hazard left most
   of the harm in place.
   - **Why it matters for the talk:** the talk's most useful
     number this week. The threat model the audience imagines
     is an attacker; the measured harm is an ordinary hazard
     the agent walked past. And the fix that worked was
     specific — name the hazard, name the consequence — not a
     generic warning.
   - **Limits:** offline rebuilds of reported incidents, a
     selected set of 108; reproduction is not the original event.
5. **Compaction is lossy — and it distorts, not just omits
   ([arXiv:2611.25673](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25673),
   Nov 19, 2026).** CompAct-Bench: a benchmark for working-context
   compaction in long-horizon agent tasks, across BrowseComp,
   SWE-bench and an internal CompanyBench. Compaction remains
   highly lossy even for frontier models; Claude- and GPT-family
   models are strongest, yet substantial degradation persists
   under aggressive compaction. Failures come not only from
   omitted information but from distorted task state — turning
   unresolved issues into apparent conclusions, or forgetting
   previously established results.
   - **Why it matters for the talk:** the mechanism behind the
     OpenAI incident, measured. The agent's context was
     compacted, and then it treated an unresolved task as
     resolved. "Need ignore" was written after the state had
     already been bent.
   - **Limits:** benchmark tasks, not deployed harm; measures
     downstream task accuracy, not whether the distortion is
     dangerous.
6. **Enforcement outside the model, over what it actually
   did ([arXiv:2611.25599](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25599),
   Nov 19, 2026).** Tether: a verified stateful policy engine
   for system-level monitoring of agents. Each proposed action
   runs in an ephemeral, isolated sandbox; Tether infers its
   semantics from observed system calls, then enforces stateful
   policies — including information-flow control across
   multi-step flows — written in Lean 4 and formally verified
   against global invariants. On realistic coding-agent
   benchmarks it blocked 100% of in-scope attacks with a benign
   false-positive rate under 5%.
   - **Why it matters for the talk:** the check sits on the
     side effects, not on the model's words. A policy that can
     say "no network access after reading a sensitive file" and
     be verified to mean it is the closest thing in this week's
     papers to a brake.
   - **Limits:** "in-scope" attacks; coding-agent benchmarks;
     someone still has to write the policies.
7. **Memory is a dose, and the dose depends on the model
   ([arXiv:2611.26361](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26361),
   Nov 19, 2026).** How Much Should an Agent Remember?
   Deployment Lessons from Self-Distilled Memory for LLM
   Agents: the pipeline mines short lessons from the agent's
   own trajectories, consolidates them into guidelines, and
   ships in the open-source altk-evolve library, deployed in
   IBM's CUGA agent and watsonx Orchestrate. The central
   finding: the right amount of memory is a property of the
   model, not the memory. DeepSeek-V3.2 gains +9.5 points of
   task completion with its full 191-guideline set; gpt-oss-120b
   reaches 51.2% with all 199 guidelines but 55.4% with a
   selected subset of about 29. The failure log is the talk
   material: a high-support guideline that backfired,
   provenance silently dropped during consolidation, and a
   swallowed API error that failed every task while producing
   a plausible log.
   - **Why it matters for the talk:** memory helps until it
     hurts, and the hurt is quiet — a plausible log over total
     failure is the record lying again, from inside the
     product.
   - **Limits:** AppWorld benchmark; the IBM deployment claim
     is the authors'.
8. **Memory as belief, not transcript
   ([arXiv:2611.25855](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25855),
   Nov 19, 2026).** HabitusMem: memory as the assistant's
   belief about the user — a latent preference state that every
   interaction reveals and updates. Each belief carries its
   evidence, its scope, and its status, so the agent can tell
   which preferences still hold and where they apply. Best
   results among memory systems on PersonaMem-v2 and
   VitaBench 2.0.
   - **Why it matters for the talk:** a memory that says what
     it believes, why, and where it applies is checkable. A
     transcript is not — and an agent acting on who you were
     is the quiet version of the same failure.
   - **Limits:** benchmark gains; the belief records are still
     model-written, so the check is only as good as the writer.

Rei's digest carries two more, both verified by me at arXiv
today. They are her picks, in her framing.

9. **A successful trace can still teach badly
   ([arXiv:2611.26510](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26510),
   Nov 19, 2026).** AutoMaster: Skill-FT — an LLM agent
   analyses teacher trajectories, writes short behavioural
   instructions ("skills") to suppress unsupported guesses,
   unproductive repetition and superficial verification, and
   revises them from rollout and student-evaluation feedback.
   Across six combinations of Qwen3.5-4B/9B and three tasks,
   test means rose 1.42–8.99 percentage points over fine-tuning
   on successful unguided traces; Terminal-Bench 2.1 for the 9B
   student moved from 26.59% to 35.58%.
   - **Why it matters for the talk (Rei's line):** a stronger
     teacher is not the only way to improve a student;
     improving the demonstrations is a separate lever. A
     completed task is a blunt label — success does not say
     which behaviours should be reinforced.
   - **Limits:** two sizes in one student family; gains not
     established at equal total compute or on other families.
10. **Fewer records and shorter records are different goals
    ([arXiv:2611.26427](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26427),
    Nov 19, 2026).** MemFolder: RecordFolder groups related
    records and merges them; ContextFolder then shortens what
    is stored and revises the encoding when a fact-recovery
    check fails. With half the Mem0 records, RecordFolder
    retained 99.0% of uncompressed average accuracy across
    three text benchmarks (60.5 vs 61.1), with mean retrieval
    speedups of 1.41–1.86×; on the multimodal web benchmark,
    ContextFolder matched LLMLingua-2's 29.47% accuracy with
    20.9 rather than 31.7 tokens per record.
    - **Why it matters for the talk (Rei's line):** measure
      retrieval latency, tokens delivered, and fact recovery
      separately. The 99% is relative benchmark accuracy, not
      99% document fidelity — and the fact check is no guarantee
      that rare commitments survive compression.
    - **Limits:** benchmark accuracy, not end-to-end speed or
      fidelity; compression can still drop what nobody thought
      to check for.
