## New agent papers

One paper this run — Rei's digest already covers the Nov 4
arXiv items, so this is the Nov 3 item she did not take.

1. **You don't need long context (Belcak et al.;
   arXiv:2611.05306, November 3, 2026).** Frequent compaction
   keeps short contexts, and frontier models sacrifice little to
   no accuracy on long-horizon and long-input tasks — including
   long-horizon software-engineering tasks run at most one action
   per context. The serving numbers: cutting the window from
   256K to 16K buys 3.6x (GLM-5.2), 5.6x (Kimi K2.6), and 7x
   (Qwen 3 Coder Next) more decoded tokens per second on
   identical hardware, with estimated cost savings of 2.2–8.8x
   (Opus 4.5) and 2.4–5.0x (GPT 5.6 Sol). The authors then
   propose Semantic State Space Models: models post-trained to
   run on an explainable natural-language state instead of a
   growing history.
   ([arXiv:2611.05306](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05306))
   Why it matters: if the agent only ever sees a compacted
   summary, the summary *is* the rulebook — and ARCBench showed
   the summary can be attacked and that a retained rule still
   loses authority. This paper says compaction is cheap and
   nearly free in accuracy; ARCBench says it is not free in
   compliance. Together they are the talk's cleanest pair: the
   engineering says compact, the safety says check what the
   compaction kept. Limits: minimal ReAct harnesses (one Bash
   action at a time); mostly coding, search, tool use, and
   long-document QA — not multi-tool, parallel, or multimodal
   agents; serving numbers are setup-specific; SSSMs are a
   proposal, not a trained model; no independent replication.
   (Abstract read from arXiv; serving figures via a secondary
   summary of the paper.)

  door asked who writes the rulebook; his Nov 5 door asks which
  road counts. Rei's fixture gives the working form: three

  checking — and an agent that read the first answer before
  writing the second has not checked anything twice.
- **Identity week is now a standards race, not a vendor
  trend.** Five "know your agent" products in about a week, and
  the newest is backed by a Chinese state academy with a path to
  the ITU. The talk should welcome all five and then ask the
  question none of them answers by itself — who holds the
  identity record, and can the agent, its installer, or its
  summary rewrite it? The blocs will disagree about the answer;
  the question is the same in every room.
- **Compaction is now both the engineering answer and the
  safety question.** "You don't need long context" makes the
  economic case for short contexts; ARCBench makes the
  compliance case against trusting them blindly; Anthropic's
  message threads make the plumbing default. The talk can put
  it in one line: the cheaper the history gets, the more the
  summary decides — so the summary is where the checking has
  to live.
