## New agent papers (all abstracts verified; announced Oct 2 – Oct 5)

1. **arXiv:2610.02702** (Oct 2, announced Oct 5) — *Silent Dissent:
   LLM Agents That Yield to the Majority Still Represent Their Original
   Premise.* Asch-style confederate peers unanimously assert a wrong
   answer; the agents that bow to the majority still represent their
   original "bridge" entity in the residual stream (J-lens) — they
   change their statement, not their mind. Pre-registered. Why it
   matters: the church-friendly "character" illustration for agent
   collectives — stated consensus in multi-agent debate and voting can
   overstate agreement; verification must go deeper than a show of
   hands. Limits: scripted peers, two-hop factual questions only;
   conformity dynamics may differ with genuinely reasoning peers.
   [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02702)
2. **arXiv:2610.01491** (Oct 1) — *Auditing Web Agent Evaluation on
   WebArena-Lite: Human Review of Outcomes and Trajectories.* Human
   audit of all 165 tasks: automatic evaluators missed 5.45–8.49
   points of real success, and the 102 failures were execution
   breakdowns (scroll loops, expired sessions, mis-clicks), not
   reasoning failures. Why it matters: the concrete story for the
   talk that today's agent gap is *doing*, not thinking — and a
   memory/analysis support mechanism lifted an untrained 9B model from
   13.9% to 18.8%. Limits: one benchmark, 165 tasks; "corrected"
   scores rest on the authors' own audit protocol.
   [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01491)
3. **arXiv:2610.01756** (Oct 1) — *SoK: Decentralized Agent Economic
   Infrastructure.* Systematizes 17 property families across 12
   systems; key concept "guarantee closure" — guarantees established at
   one stage must actually constrain later stages. Documents recurring
   verification-to-settlement failures (valid evidence ignored,
   conforming work unaccepted). Why it matters: the formal counterpart
   to the house's checkout worry — "a workflow can look correct at each
   step and still produce the wrong outcome"; trust is end-to-end, not
   local. Limits: systematization, no new experiments; controlled
   workflows, not production agent economies.
   [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01756)
4. **arXiv:2610.01195** (Oct 1) — *Federated Agent Optimization.*
   Formalizes how distributed agents learn from each other's experience
   (memory, tools, skills, rewards) without sharing raw data — a
   multi-objective trade-off among utility, privacy leakage, and
   communication cost. Why it matters: the trust story for the talk's
   thesis — since experience can't (and shouldn't) be pooled centrally,
   federated architectures are where trust has to live. Limits:
   formulation/survey; no experiments; privacy-utility claims are
   conceptual.
   [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01195)
5. **arXiv:2610.00906** (Oct 1) — *ActiveSaddler: Automated Curriculum
   Learning for Agent Harness Optimization.* Co-evolves the training
   curriculum (a failure-pattern bandit) with the agent harness itself
   — not just how the harness updates, but which scenarios train it:
   +4.4/+7.5 points on GAIA2 and Terminal-Bench 2.0. Why it matters:
   third data point for the "architecture, not vibes" engineering
   section (with Mingbird and VISTA) — capability currently lives in
   the harness. Limits: two benchmarks; gains measured against one
   fixed-order baseline, generality untested.
   [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00906)

   Skipped: 2610.03174 (finance deep-research agent), 2610.02924

   2610.02847 (MARL credit assignment, non-LLM), 2610.02951 (MoE
   expert pruning), 2610.03319 (UAV swarm security), 2610.02298
   (3D-editing benchmark) — domain, niche, or not agent-relevant.
