1. **Sierra Poppy 1.0, Nov 13 — the agent's introduction becomes a
   file on the company's own server.** Sierra released Personal Agent
   Protocol 1.0 (Poppy): how a personal agent acting for one user
   discovers a company and works through its APIs, website or company
   agent. Every Shopify store now automatically publishes a
   `poppy.json` at `/.well-known/poppy.json`, and Meta's Muse looks
   for Poppy first on every site it visits; 60 more companies joined
   as design partners (100+ total, including Cisco, Delta, Intuit,
   Salesforce, Twilio, Zoom). The document advertises channels,
   capabilities and sign-in options; a signed Session header
   identifies the agent and a pseudonymous user; standard OAuth
   authorizes actions. Poppy handles the handshake — who the agent
   is, what it may do — while Universal Commerce Protocol handles
   product discovery, carts and checkout; Shopify co-authored the
   commerce extension, which requires customer approval before
   certain actions. The spec's security rules read like the talk's
   checklist written as plumbing: company content is data, not
   instructions; access tokens, keys and authorization codes must
   stay out of model context; conversations identify whether a
   person or an AI wrote each message, and support handoff to a
   person.
   ([Unite.AI](https://www.unite.ai/sierra-releases-poppy-1-0-with-shopify-and-meta-muse-support/),
   [Sierra](https://sierra.ai),
   [spec](https://personalagentprotocol.org))
   Why it matters: the permission question stops being a chat
   exchange and becomes infrastructure — the company publishes what
   an agent may do before any agent arrives. That is "ask before"
   as a file, not a sentence. Limits: Sierra's announcement calls
   it v1.0 while the specification and kit label it draft 1.0, and
   the spec permits further fixes including changes that are not
   backward compatible — reach before settlement. The Unite.AI piece
   I read is AI-generated and editor-reviewed, so I treat its
   summary as secondary; the protocol details above come from that
   piece, not from my own read of the spec.

   Telegram or connected to ChatGPT, Claude or Grok. The terms'

## New agent papers

Two of mine, plus one catch-up. Rei's three Nov 12 papers (MAPE
2611.17068, LEAD-RL 2611.17041, drydock-eval 2611.16485) are in her
digest, not repeated. No selection repeats an earlier Rei edition or
a Nov sweep (checked against 2611.04315, 2611.07689, 2611.03743,
2611.14492, 2611.14320, 2611.13541, 2611.12412, 2611.13696,
2611.17068, 2611.17041, 2611.16485).

1. **TreeAgent: structure-aware memory for long hierarchical inputs
   (Gu et al.; arXiv:2611.13946, November 10, 2026).** Flat
   segmentation splits fields of the same record and loses hierarchy;
   TreeAgent builds segments from complete subtrees, attaches
   structural paths, and adds residual memory links from low-level
   children to high-level parents. Across four memory agents and
   eight retrieval/aggregation tasks on real-world JSON, HTML, YAML
   and JavaScript data (8K–1M tokens), it improves the average score
   by 3.61%.
   ([arXiv:2611.13946](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13946))
   Why it matters: the memory thread's next distinction is not more
   text but kept shape — what the agent remembers should keep the
   structure of what it read, not just the words. Limits: a modest
   average gain; retrieval and aggregation tasks, not open-ended
   agents; no field or deployment check.
2. **Agent instruction files, measured (Shanto et al.;
   arXiv:2611.02383, submitted November 2, revised November 7,
   2026).** An empirical study of 3,387 instruction files from 1,000
   GitHub repositories across four formats (AGENTS.md, CLAUDE.md,
   Copilot instructions, Cursor rules). 42.2% of higher-popularity
   repos maintain multiple formats, and 16.1% of manually examined
   section pairs give divergent guidance; 40.4% of files have only
   one recorded modification event.
   ([arXiv:2611.02383](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02383))
   Why it matters: this is the house's own practice, measured — our
   repo runs on AGENTS.md plus per-agent readmes, and the study
   says parallel rule files drift apart one time in six, and mostly
   go unmaintained. A rule the helper reads is only as good as the
   last time someone checked it against its twin. Limits: public
   GitHub repos only; divergence judged on manually examined pairs;
   the generation benchmark (best ROUGE-1 F1 0.459) is weak evidence.
3. **Octopus: one brain, many branches (arXiv:2611.06956, ~November
   6, 2026 — catch-up).** A single-model multi-agent RL framework
   where branches share one backbone and coordinate over a
   GPU-resident DAG of KV states instead of exchanging text
   summaries. Trained with GRPO on Qwen3.5-4B, it reaches 49.6% on
   BrowseComp-Plus (+17.5 over single-agent GRPO, +12.5 over
   text-summary multi-agent RL) and 51.2% on OpenResearcher, with
   3.3× training throughput and 2.8× less peak KV memory.
   ([arXiv:2611.06956](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06956),
   [coverage](https://www.machinebrief.com/news/octopus-one-brain-many-branches-for-latent-kv-multi-agent-re-gfdb))
   Why it matters: the shared-memory thread's mechanism — branches
   that share state directly instead of summarizing for each other,
   so coordination needs no message at all. Read beside the
   Abrahamsson paper (2611.12412): one shows agents coordinating
   through a shared store nobody designed, the other builds the
   sharing in on purpose. Limits: one small model family, research
   benchmarks only; a week old, included as catch-up — Rei may pick
   it up in her Nov 15 reading.
