1. **CrossProfile: A Diagnostic Benchmark for LLM Group Decision
   Memory (Imel, Charradi, Sghir, El Khammar; posted Oct 27–28 per
   secondary reporting; arXiv ID unconfirmed).**
   Secondary reports (AIWeekly, Nowness listing) describe a benchmark
   of 60 templates / 480 tasks with automatically verified answer
   keys, crossing ownership of supporting, decoy and irrelevant facts
   while holding the text fixed, in a hidden-profile setup: the
   correct decision needs facts no single participant holds. Findings
   as reported: picking memory by relevance loses 23–31 points to
   rarest-first selection in the classic hidden-profile
   configuration — but rarest-first itself does no better than
   random on average; removing or randomizing attribution tags
   raises classic-configuration accuracy by 13–24 points, exposing
   a social-proof bias (facts attributed to many agents get extra
   weight); and the complete transcript yields lower accuracy than
   no notes at all. "Accuracy follows the balance of evidence the
   memory keeps, not the policy that kept it."
   ([AIWeekly report](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/crossprofile-paper-memory-heuristics-fail-llm-group-calls))
   Why it matters, if it verifies: it is the group version of the
   Anthropic study. One agent's lean persuades one analyst; here, a
   group's memory persuades its own decision model, by counting
   heads instead of weighing evidence — and keeping everything
   (the full transcript) is worse than keeping nothing, which is
   the memory chapter's sharpest counter-slogan to "keep the
   record." It also bites the Salon: a compiled lexicon is a memory
   policy, and this paper says the policy matters less than the
   balance of evidence it happens to keep.
   Limits: secondary reporting only, ID unconfirmed, primary
   abstract not read; small models (Qwen2.5-7B, Llama-3.1-8B, two
   DeepSeek models); synthetic hidden-profile tasks, not deployed
   multi-agent work; the "worse than no notes" result is on this
   benchmark's decision model, not a general law.
