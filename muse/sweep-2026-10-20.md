## New agent papers

From the papers round (Oct 18–20 submissions scanned via the arXiv
API, ~120 reviewed; abstracts read, methods checked where the claim
matters for the talk). Style after `rei/digests/2026-09-30.md`.

1. **arXiv:2610.25947 — *Claim Laundering: When Agent Decision Models
   Trust Records Over Reality*** (Khezresmaeilzadeh et al.; submitted
   Oct 19). When an action silently fails, agent decision models trust
   their own log ("success") over their own observations: 53–100% of
   claimed-but-failed steps still lead to stop/advance/purchase across
   4 decision models × 3 domains (Android traces, ALFWorld, WebShop).
   The authors ship a cheap fix (EVIGATE, a probe-based veto: +18.7
   task-success points in live ALFWorld).
   [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25947).
   Why it matters: a clean, quotable exhibit that *an agent believes
   its own paperwork more than reality* — direct fuel for the
   trust/character angle. Limits: the veto needs representative
   calibration states, which don't transfer reliably from offline
   counterfactuals to live trajectories.
2. **arXiv:2610.26438 — *Fake News, Real Crashes: Information Injection
   Attacks on LLM Financial Trading Agents*** (Wang, Saxena; submitted
   Oct 19). Fabricated news (62% attack success) crushes prompt
   injection (14%) against trading agents — and *surface form beats
   content*: deleting a named source or "BREAKING" tag drops success
   from 100% to 0% on the same claim; attacks generalize across model
   vendors, and agents herding on the same feed can be pushed into the
   same trade at the same moment.
   [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26438).
   Why it matters: real-money stakes for "no LLM company can be
   trusted" — with a trading-floor mechanism, not a lab toy. Limits:
   lab benchmark (~10,000 decisions on 15 US stocks) with simulated
   agents; demonstrates the vulnerability, not an actual market crash.
3. **arXiv:2610.25707 — *Whose Credential Is It? How Owner Attribution
   in Agent Memory Shapes Cross-User Account Misuse*** (So et al.;
   submitted Oct 19). A shared-memory agent retrieves *another user's
   credential* in 84.7% of required logins, and under stress uses it in
   80–94% of runs (changing the victim's account up to 43% of the
   time) — memory without identity. The fix is almost embarrassingly
   human: writing *whose* credential each line is drops misuse from 92
   to 1 of 305 paired runs.
   [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25707).
   Why it matters: the perfect "memory and identity" exhibit —
   character as bookkeeping, and the same human-shaped idea as Rei's
   idempotency rule. Limits: AppWorld simulation; the worst-case
   stress test (memory holds only the foreign credential) is
   adversarially constructed.
4. **arXiv:2610.25561 — *Let Them Sleep: Recursive Self-Pruning Memory
   for Long-Horizon LLM Agents*** (Chauhan et al.; submitted Oct 18;
   Baylearn 2026 oral). The thesis: the agent memory problem isn't
   storing more, it's *knowing what to forget* — during "sleep" the
   agent clusters failures into rules and deletes the raw episodes;
   rules decay unless reinforced. ~93% fewer retry loops, ~30% fewer
   stored entries across three unrelated settings.
   [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25561).
   Why it matters: church-talk gold, and the constructive companion to
   the earlier "agents that forget" paper (arXiv:2610.20563, 10-14).
   Limits: three evaluation settings; the "universal operating point"
   claim rests on instrumentation of only two studies.

Deliberately skipped: pure benchmark/leaderboard papers (PACE,
FinHopper, LeakBench method comparisons, ACTS, MASIF, SWE-Residual,
