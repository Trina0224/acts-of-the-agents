## Papers (new — read at arXiv)

Gemini's Nov 17 brief already covers Soteria (2611.17880),
Authorization Drift (2611.09113), and intent-graph auditing
(2611.11305); not repeated here. The multi-agent synthesis
(2611.17610) was covered in the Nov 16 sweep, which is re-pushed
with this batch.

1. **WorkflowArena: the best AI team finishes barely a third
   of its work ([arXiv:2611.12856](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12856),
   Nov 9, 2026).** 145 long-horizon tasks across 8 professional
   domains, each an integrated deliverable with interdependent
   components, scored by a hidden verifier; agents decide
   autonomously whether and how to delegate, and every delegation
   is recorded. The strongest configuration — Claude Opus 5.5 in
   Claude Code at max effort — resolves only 36.95% of tasks on
   average across domains, and no configuration is best in every
   domain. Holding the executor fixed, workflows authored by Opus
   raise weaker executors' scores by 6.41 points on average:
   organizing the work is itself a measurable skill, distinct
   from doing it.
   - **Why it matters for the talk:** delegation is the skill
     nobody benchmarked, and the best team in the world finishes
     barely a third of its work. It cuts against "more agents =
     better" — the question is not how many agents, but whether
     anyone is organizing them.
   - **Limits:** authored professional artifacts, not live
     jobs; 36.95% is an average across domains, not a single pass
     rate; harness confound (Claude Code vs Codex) is baked into
     the configurations.
2. **Long-horizon agents fail with the evidence already in the
   room ([arXiv:2611.13254](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13254),
   Nov 9, 2026, Villanova).** A trajectory-centred study of 95
   failed runs of six multi-agent systems (Who&When and MAD
   corpora), reading each trajectory in full. Faults enter early
   — median at 18% of the run — mostly as wrong decisions or
   fabrications over task state. In 72% of failed runs the
   trajectory already contained evidence against the error before
   the failure; in 88% of those, no agent acted on it. Something
   flagged a deviation in 31% of runs, but no response removed
   the deviated state. Restoring the agent's own state would have
   sufficed in 35 of 95 runs.
   - **Why it matters for the talk:** the failure is not missing
     evidence — it is evidence ignored. It is the empirical
     version of the talk's "who checks" thread: a check that ran
     and flagged, and still nothing changed.
   - **Limits:** hand-annotated failure corpora, 95 runs; 45%
     of the hand-crafted Who&When failures ended in a provider
     content-filter crash after which the harness emitted a final
     answer no agent produced — a harness artifact, not an agent
     error.
3. **Phantom Consensus: agreement is not evidence
   ([arXiv:2611.08971](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08971), Nov 6,
   2026; NeurIPS 2026 SocialAgent Workshop).** PHANTOM-Bench
   separates agents' isolated private judgments from their public
   decisions after ambiguous social signals. Ambiguous public
   signals consistently degrade decision quality and can turn
   correct private group judgments into incorrect public
   collective outcomes: public accuracy drops by as much as 47.8
   percentage points, collective rationality failures reach 47.3%
   in the most susceptible setting. Revealing the agents'
   recorded private decisions repairs many failures without
   revealing the gold label.
   - **Why it matters for the talk:** a group of agents can be
     confidently wrong together. "Show me the private judgments
     behind the agreement" is a check a church audience can
     remember — and it is the same move as Claude opening a source
     that wasn't his own.
   - **Limits:** controlled synthetic collectives, not deployed
     systems; susceptibility varies by model; nine models tested.
