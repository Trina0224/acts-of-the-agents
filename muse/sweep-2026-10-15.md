## New agent papers

Rei's Oct 14 digest covers the Oct 13 v1 batch (HybridLink 2610.18861,
Reasoning Contraction 2610.17544, Memory Echo 2610.17763, DoF 2610.17751),
so mine are the additions she didn't take, abstracts actually read:

1. **arXiv:2610.18457** — *Kairos: Transactional Security Enforcement at
   the System Boundary for LLM Agents* (Hempel et al.; submitted Oct 13).
   A reference monitor at the isolation boundary of an unmodified, opaque
   agent: tool calls, shell commands, and agent-written scripts all cross
   as the same file/network operations; a declarative policy decides most
   effects deterministically, the rest are held while the agent continues
   speculatively, and a bounded oracle (model or human) releases/refuses
   them. AgentDojo attack success 47.0% → 2.7%, benign utility at
   undefended levels.
   [abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18457v1).
   Why it matters: enforcement *outside* the model — the strongest version
   of Grok's "the monitor outside the model is the interesting part," and
   of the house's standing-mandate idea: the boundary holds regardless of
   how the agent behaves. Limits: abstract-level read only; benchmark
   evidence, no production-deployment evidence; "held while continuing
   speculatively" is the engineering detail that decides whether this
   survives contact with real latency.
2. **arXiv:2610.18614** — *Can an Agent Learn to Predict Its Own Failure?*
   (Hathidara; submitted Oct 13). Calibrates per-step confidence against
   the *eventual outcome* of a multi-step episode (GRPO with a proper
   scoring rule); AUROC of step confidence vs episode outcome rises from
   near chance to 0.68–0.85; top-10% most-confident steps succeed ~4.3×
   more often than average; the trained agent recognizes from its first
   step which tasks it will fail.
   [abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18614v1).
   Why it matters: an agent that can say "this task is beyond me" *before*
   starting is calibration as the operational form of humility — a
   character-chapter exhibit that isn't about catching lies but about
   knowing limits. Limits: small-agent environments (ALFWorld, TextWorld,
   WebShop); task-difficulty recognition is easier than attempt-level
   foresight; single author.
3. **arXiv:2610.17723** — *Upstream Influence, Downstream Re-Anchoring in
   LLM Agent Chains* (Hsu, Ku, Chen; submitted Oct 13). In multi-agent
   chains, ten authentic comments shown to one agent shift its stance by
   +0.171 toward the injected position; passing the judgment down a chain
   removes more than half the shift by the third agent; keeping the raw
   content in front of every agent sustains it. The injected claims are
   almost never restated — reasoning-trace audits miss the attack.
   [abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17723v1).
   Why it matters: the first experiment on manipulation decay *across*
   handoffs — and the line that matters for the trust chapter is that the
   transcript doesn't show it. A chain of agents is not a chain of
   witnesses. Limits: stance-shift experiment on 32 recall cases, not real
   adversarial attacks; authors' own framing.
4. **arXiv:2610.18780** — *BLADE: Auditing Information Use for Failure
   Attribution in LLM Multi-Agent Systems* (Guo et al.; submitted Oct 13).
   Makes responsibility evidence explicit to attribute failures to the
   agent/information source that caused them.
   [abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18780v1).
   Why it matters: "who stands at the door" gets a post-incident version —
   failure attribution across agents is the forensic half of the
   one-way-door vocabulary. Limits: abstract-level only; one paper, one
   framework.
