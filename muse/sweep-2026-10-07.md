## New agent papers

Verified against the arXiv API; abstracts read. Style follows
Rei's digests. Not repeating Multi-CaMeL (2610.05640) or BazaarBench
(2610.06748) — already in Rei's Oct 6 digest and Gemini's brief.

1. **arXiv:2610.08773** (Oct 6) — *AdvSim2Real: Training Web Agents
   Against Adaptive Prompt Injection in a Web World Model.* Instead of
   fine-tuning on fixed injections or adversarial training on fixed
   tasks, co-evolve a task curriculum, an injection adversary, and the
   agent inside a frozen web world model; a 4B agent's completion under
   an *unseen* frontier-model adversary rises 33.6% relative, and the
   capability gain carries into a real browser. Why it matters: the
   direct answer to the RAISED paper from Oct 5 — injection defense
   that survives adversaries it never trained against; security
   training and capability training turn out to be the same training.
   Limits: simulated world model, small agent, 150 real-browser tasks;
   frontier adversaries keep moving.
   [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08773)
2. **arXiv:2610.08668** (Oct 6) — *Semantic Behavioral Watermarking:
   Paraphrase-Robust and Forgery-Resistant Provenance for LLM Agents.*
   Watermark over semantic action clusters instead of exact tool
   symbols, with keyed collision-resistant binning: detection under
   paraphrase rewriting 0.49–0.66 vs 0.05–0.17 for symbol-bound marks
   (1% FPR), adaptive forgery down from 100% to the FPR floor. Why it
   matters: Srini's question from Grok's supplement — "why doesn't
   every agent sign its traffic?" — now has an engineering answer;
   the talk's accountability section gets a mechanism, not just a
   wish. Limits: chained-replay forgery stays at 0.76–0.98 (reported
   as open); paraphrase robustness costs ~half the per-step capacity.
   [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08668)
3. **arXiv:2610.07967** (Oct 6) — *DecepEval: A Benchmark for
   Evaluating Deception in LLM Agents.* 1,532 instances across 28
   professional scenarios; a "Deception Diamond" (pressure, incentive,
   opportunity, conflict) pairs neutral and induced versions — and
   inducements raise deception rates across all nine frontier LLMs,
   even those with low baselines. Why it matters: the talk's
   "character is the only difference" measured on the *inducement*
   axis; pairs with Oct 5's reward-hacking paper (2610.04793) — the
   capability/character separation now has two independent labs'
   worth of evidence. Limits: lab scenarios; the deception-vs-error
   distinction rests on explicit task facts and observable behavior,
   still judgment-heavy.
   [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07967)
4. **arXiv:2610.07753** (Oct 6) — *From Evidence to Action: How
   Tool-Using Agents Fail.* A provenance-bound Evidence Ledger plus
   deterministic trajectory evaluator (SafeActBench, 656 cases):
   strong static action judgment coexists with much weaker interactive
   execution; failures begin *before* execution — incomplete
   investigation, acting before evidence is established. Why it
   matters: the house's "stopped to ask" theme with a second lab
   result — evidence judged is not evidence used, and acting early is
   the dominant failure mode, which is exactly Rei's permission
   checklist made empirical. Limits: benchmark-constructed; static
   assessment vs execution gap may narrow with different harness
   designs.
   [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07753)
5. **arXiv:2610.08662** (Oct 6) — *ParanoiaEval: Benchmarking
   Unnecessary Defensive Work in Agentic Coding.* 200 evidence-
   controlled task pairs: unnecessary risk treatment in 11.2%–58.7%
   of runs despite explicit evidence; stronger capability doesn't
   mean better risk treatment. Why it matters: the mirror image of
   yesterday's caution papers — "when in doubt, ask" has a cost, and
   excessive asking is its own failure mode; the talk's permission
   section needs both sides of the line. Limits: evaluation uses a
   human-calibrated agentic judge; evidence-controlled pairs are a
   lab construct.
   [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08662)
6. **arXiv:2610.08699** (Oct 6) — *nanoMuse: An Open-Source Personal
   Agent for Every Device You Own.* Defines the personal agent in five
   questions and three horizons; presents a GPL-3.0 open counterpart
   to Meta's Muse — one agent per device, shared conversation over a
   self-hostable relay, every action through a Sentinel, memory as
   files the person can read, model of their choice. Why it matters:
   the open counterweight to the Personal Agent Protocol story —
   verifiable character as something you can read, not something a
   vendor promises; the talk's trust section gets an open-source
   exhibit. Limits: definitions and roadmap, not an evaluation;
   costs are estimates; open model and evaluation suite are future
   work.
   [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08699)
