## New agent papers (all abstracts verified, submitted Sept 30 – Oct 2)

1. **arXiv:2610.00972** (Oct 1) — *VeriHarness: Scaling Agentic
   Verification for Long-Horizon Tasks.* Consensus can conceal errors;
   disagreement exposes correct alternatives. Turns the base model into
   an agentic verifier with its own workspace: a disagreement resolver
   checks competing claims against environmental evidence, a consensus
   challenger tests shared claims. +6.2/+6.4 points over a single
   rollout (Gemini 3.5 Flash / Claude Opus 4.8), on five long-horizon
   workspace benchmarks. Why it matters: the formal counterpart to the
   house's own discipline — verification as an agent that must show
   its evidence, not a bigger model. Limits: five benchmarks, ~$100k
   of rollouts; no deployed-system study.
   [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00972)
2. **arXiv:2610.00710** (Sept 30) — *ReLiveGym: Evaluating Long-Lived
   Agents over Weeks of Replayed Reality.* Agents act sparsely over
   simulated weeks of replayed news/market/social streams; the key
   finding is that *when to act* is a harness-design axis, and the
   optimal design varies by task and model. Why it matters: the first
   benchmark that asks the talk's real long-horizon question — not
   "can it finish a task" but "should it act now or wait." Limits:
   simulated weeks, not real ones; diagnostic, not a deployed
   long-lived agent.
   [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00710)
3. **arXiv:2610.02001** (Oct 1) — *Mingbird: A Local-First Agent
   Harness Enabling Small Open Models to Complete Real Tasks.* Many
   "small models can't do agents" failures are the harness's fault:
   byte-level prefill budget, finish gate, and loop detection lift
   2B–35B open models from 0.631 to 0.886 on the same 18 real tasks.
   Why it matters: live ammunition for the commoditization claim —
   the difference lives in the harness, and in how seriously it's
   engineered. Limits: self-built benchmark (LRAB), single machine,
   single-trial scoring; ±0.069 ablation noise by the authors' own
   flag.
   [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02001)
4. **arXiv:2610.02005** (Oct 1) — *Counting Moves, Weighing Voices:
   Bayesian Dialectical Argumentation for Calibrated Multi-LLM
   Councils.* A multi-agent "council" that treats each agent's
   proposals and challenges as evidence about *that agent's*
   reliability, then *inverts* persistently unreliable agents rather
   than merely outvoting them. Why it matters: a structural answer
   to "when capability becomes a commodity, character is the only
   difference." Limits: calibration claims on QA-style benchmarks,
   not consequential agentic decisions; adversary persistence is
   modeled, not observed.
   [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02005)
5. **arXiv:2610.02070** (Oct 1) — *Causal Memory Policy: Making
   Memory Utility Identifiable by Intervening on Retrieval.* A memory
   that is never retrieved has *unidentifiable* utility — 54% of
   required memories on LongMemEval, 67% on LoCoMo. Why it matters:
   punctures agent-memory hype honestly: most memory systems cannot
   measure whether their memories matter — the "don't trust the
   marketing" beat on memory. Limits: causal-inference heavy, hard
   to translate for a non-technical audience; modest gains.
   [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02070)
6. **arXiv:2610.02163** (Oct 2) — *AutoCompact: Learning When to
   Compact Context in Long-Horizon Coding Agents.* Trains the agent to
   manage its own forgetting — when to compact, what state to preserve
   — with judge-corrected trajectories + outcome RL: +9.2pp on
   SWE-bench Verified (fixed-threshold compaction actually
   *underperforms* the base model). Why it matters: agents must
   manage forgetting, not just remembering. Limits: trained at 32K,
   evaluated at 256K; one scaffold, generalization unproven.
   [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02163)
7. **arXiv:2610.02200** (Oct 1) — *VISTA: A Visual Harness for
   Reasoning in an Interactive World.* Lossless visual memory lets
   Claude Opus 5.0 go from 40.68 to a perfect 100.00 Relative Human
   Action Efficiency on ARC-AGI-3, using 57% fewer actions than
   first-time humans. Why it matters: second independent data point
   (with Mingbird) that the harness, not the model, is where agentic
   capability currently lives. Limits: puzzle/game environments,
   soft human baseline (first-time players).
   [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02200)
8. **arXiv:2610.02202** (Oct 1) — *ScholarCatalyst: A Benchmark for
   Retrieving Papers That Inspire New Research.* Honest negative
   result: agentic search does *no better* than plain embedding
   retrieval (0.42 vs 0.48 Recall@20) at finding prior papers that
   actually inspired completed projects; even a Claude Fable
   5.1-based agent barely reaches 0.51. Why it matters: the ready-made
   "agents are not yet wise" anecdote for the talk's humility beat.
   Limits: retrieval benchmark with an agent twist, not an agent
   capability study; inspiration labels are retrospective and recall-
   biased.
   [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02202)
