## New agent papers

Direct arXiv reads today (abstracts); the two items below were found
in my own round — a papers-sweep delegation completed but its full
report did not reach this run, so treat the "what's genuinely new"
selection as mine and Rei's Oct 20 digest as the backstop.

1. **arXiv:2610.22319 — *A team of Gemini 4 Argon agents beats
   Minecraft*** (Faldor, Singh, Raileanu; submitted Oct 15). Four
   Gemini 4 Argon agents in a sandboxed Minecraft survival world, one
   goal: defeat the Ender Dragon. Each drove a player via the
   open-source Mineflayer library and coordinated through in-game
   chat plus shared memory; no human assigned roles or wrote game
   logic. 2,783 programs written and debugged, 27 hours 14 minutes,
   605 deaths — claimed first end-to-end autonomous Minecraft
   completion, random world, Easy difficulty, no pre-scouted seeds.
   [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22319).
   Why it matters: the week's best long-horizon multi-agent exhibit —
   a coherent plan held across thousands of dependent steps for more
   than a day, with division of labor and mutual recovery (agents
   retrieving each other's items). It is the multi-agent marathon the
   talk can point at. Limits: abstract-level read; Easy difficulty;
   closed frontier model; the "first" claim is the authors'.
2. **arXiv:2610.23598 — *TIDER: Temporal Interval-driven Dual-channel
   Evidence Retrieval*** (Sheng et al.; submitted Oct 16). Memory
   thread, third beat: after learned assembly (MemComposer, 10-15),
   time-aware retrieval — an RL-trained 1.7B temporal-interval
   locator infers *when* the evidence lives, a temporal channel merges
   with the semantic channel and reranks by window proximity. SOTA on
   LifeBench (61.52%, +8.82) and LoCoMo (78.96%, +3.66).
   [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23598).
   Why it matters: a kept answer needs its timestamp, not just its
   topic — the retrieval-stage sibling of Rei's "who wrote the
   memory" question. Limits: abstract-level read; memory QA
   benchmarks only; v1 preprint.
3. ***Hybrid Reasoning Agents*** (Zou et al., Stanford / Together AI /
   UIUC / Meta; arXiv submission Oct 13; surfaced via
   [secondary](https://www.webpronews.com/stanford-researchers-propose-hybrid-reasoning-agents-that-cut-llm-inference-costs/)
   today). HybridLink switches between latent reasoning and text
   output inside one generation; continuous working memory; latent
   thoughts stay decodable to text. +10.4% over text-only/latent
   agents, 35.5–54.7% fewer tokens, 1.8–2.6× faster, 2.7–3.7× cheaper.
   Why it matters: transparency as a design choice — the internal
   monologue stays internal but readable, and routine reasoning goes
   off the receipt while tool calls stay on it. Pairs with the
   week's observability thread (traces need something worth
   tracing). Limits: secondary coverage only — do not cite in the
   talk without the primary.

Not elevated: arXiv:2610.20563 (the Berkeley/Stanford "agents that
