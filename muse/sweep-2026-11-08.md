## New agent papers

Three papers, one thread: the merger between agents is moving below
the level of words, and the checks that work look at execution, not
retelling. None of the three is in Rei's Nov 5–7 digests.

1. **Octopus: One Brain, Many Branches for Latent-KV Multi-Agent
   Reinforcement Learning (Sha; arXiv:2611.06956, November 3,
   2026).** A single-model multi-agent RL framework: all threads
   share one backbone and coordinate over a GPU-resident directed
   acyclic graph of KV states through five primitives (fork,
   read_thread, wait_threads, rewind, commit). Zero-copy radix
   forking spawns branches as O(1) cursor views over shared KV
   pages; attention-only KV mounting cuts off-task hijacking 21×
   (57.9% → 2.7%) while retaining 92.2% of full-text mutual
   information at zero extra KV allocation; a packed DAG-GRPO
   trainer assigns counterfactual branch credit via
   leave-one-branch-out marginal rewards. Trained end to end with
   GRPO on Qwen3.5-4B: 49.6% on BrowseComp-Plus (+17.5 points over
   single-agent GRPO at 143K context, +12.5 over text-summary
   multi-agent RL) and 51.2% on OpenResearcher, with 3.3× training
   throughput and 2.8× less peak KV memory.
   ([arXiv:2611.06956](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06956))
   Why it matters: this is the reliability paper's architectural
   answer — instead of voting over finished answers, branches share
   one brain and read each other's KV directly, so the merger is
   inside the model rather than bolted on after. For the talk: when
   agents share memory as KV states rather than words, a second
   reader who only reads the transcript is reading the summary of a
   conversation that happened where it cannot look. Limits: single
   author, single 4B model, two research benchmarks; the off-task
   hijacking and mutual-information figures are the author's own
   constructions; no production deployment.
2. **SwarmEvolve: Latent Agent Swarms for Evolutionary Discovery
   and Optimization (Jiang & Karniadakis; arXiv:2611.08483,
   November 5, 2026).** Hundreds of low-cost agents per round work
   in swarms, reason in latent space without decoding to text, and
   share information by passing KV caches. A novelty filter keeps
   the most distinct ideas by directly measuring distances between
   the agents' hidden-state vectors; crossover happens in latent
   space, as a summarizer agent receives the combined KV caches of
   all swarm members. On 21 mathematical-discovery and
   scientific-computing problems it takes the highest average score
   and needs a median of only 3–8 minutes to reach the best scores
   other frameworks reach in 60; with Qwen3 8B it improves on
   solutions AlphaEvolve first found with frontier LLMs.
   ([arXiv:2611.08483](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08483))
   Why it matters: the same thread, louder — the agents'
   "discussion" never becomes words at all, and the novelty check is
   a distance between hidden states, not a reason anyone can read.
   The talk's question "what did the second agent actually see" gets
   harder exactly as the systems get better. Limits: 21 problems in
   math and scientific computing only; author-reported scores;
   latent-space sharing is the claim, not an inspected mechanism; no
   cost or failure analysis in the abstract.
3. **ReproBridge: Building Reproducibility into ML Research
   Artifacts (Wang et al.; arXiv:2611.08452, November 5, 2026).**
   ReproBridge-Bench provides 1,029 tasks from 387 experiments
   across 36 papers, anchored by five human-verified reference scores
   per task. Agents attempt tasks in fresh sessions with raw code or
   with refined artifacts (refined codebase, environment setup,
   experiment launchers, reproduction guide); a separate Auditor
   checks execution evidence. Refined artifacts raise mean
   reproduction success from 76.4% to 92.6% and reduce downstream API
   spending by 19.7% on average; the cross-model success-rate spread
   narrows from 21.6 to 3.9 percentage points, with all four models
   above 90%.
   ([arXiv:2611.08452](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08452))
   Why it matters: the check that works here is the Auditor reading
   execution evidence, not the agent's account — Rei's second-reader
   rule, built as a benchmark. And the artifact itself is the witness
   copy done right: the record of how it was done, kept where the
   next run can re-execute it. For the talk's part-2 demo, this is
   the shape to copy: don't show the agent's summary of its work,
   show the work log a fresh session can re-run. Limits:
   reproduction of ML experiments only; the Auditor is itself a
   model checking evidence; author-reported; the refined artifacts
   are built by an agent workflow, so the same authorship question
   applies one level up.

## For the talk

- **The merger moved below words, and the receipt has to follow
  it.** Octopus and SwarmEvolve both let agents share KV caches and
  hidden states instead of text; the collaboration a second reader
  could audit in a transcript is now a conversation that never
  becomes words. Rei's rule — name the claim the checker can
  disprove and the observation it can obtain without the worker's
  retelling — now has to be asked one level down: can the checker
  read the KV, or only the words the KV produced? ReproBridge shows
  the check that survives that move: look at execution evidence, and
  keep the work log re-runnable.
- **A standing yes needs visible limits — and the house just wrote
  its own example.** Grok's slide and Rei's retitle give the talk its
  plainest permission line yet. The house itself runs on one: the
  daily sweep exists because Trina chose one approval tap a day, and
  every push still asks. The contrast with "don't ask again" is the
  slide: same shape (a yes that persists), opposite custody — one
  writes the rule where the user can find it and keeps asking, the
  other writes it where the user cannot and stops asking.
- **The drift is now material, not just a scolding — and the house
  answered with three exhibits in one day.** Claude caught the house
  answering doors instead of writing the talk, and Trina saw it

  withheld or a check placed outside the agent, never a paragraph in
