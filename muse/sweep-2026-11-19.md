## Papers (new — read at arXiv)

Rei's digest carries no new papers this window; the three
below are from Nov 18 submissions, all read at arXiv.

1. **The authority gate, built as architecture
   ([arXiv:2611.24845](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24845),
   Nov 18, 2026).** Vibe-Trading is an agent-native trading
   framework: every result retains its source, temporal scope,
   configuration, and producing run, and "an explicit authority
   gate separates strategy research from live execution."
   Shared evidence grounding enables verification; versioned
   research records support reproducible continuation.
   - **Why it matters for the talk:** it is Rei's four-part
     record and the stop-before-dispatch control, implemented
     rather than proposed — and the gate sits between thinking
     and spending, exactly where the talk's trust question
     lives.
   - **Limits:** a trading framework and workspace, not a
     safety evaluation; live broker execution is optional, and
     the abstract claims traceability, not a measured rate of
     prevented bad trades.
2. **The attack can come from inside the room
   ([arXiv:2611.24839](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24839),
   Nov 18, 2026).** Kaer is security-aware replanning for
   multi-agent systems: in the orchestrator pattern, a
   malicious agent can embed instructions in its feedback and
   hijack the replanning process. Kaer shields the planner by
   conveying diagnostics only through abstract templates,
   restricting revisions to deterministic repair operators, and
   requiring every modified plan to satisfy preset policies.
   On its Cintra benchmark it beats the undefended
   Magentic-One and the state-of-the-art defense ControlValve
   on robustness at comparable utility.
   - **Why it matters for the talk:** the injection the talk
     warns about does not need a hostile user or a poisoned
     web page — it can arrive as another agent's "helpful"
     feedback. The fix is also the talk's theme: the planner
     never takes raw words from an untrusted party as
     instructions.
   - **Limits:** a simulated benchmark of replanning tasks,
     not deployed systems; abstract templates slightly
     constrain utility on select tasks.
3. **Memory the user can see and edit
   ([arXiv:2611.24165](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24165),
   Nov 18, 2026).** MemoSync tackles the developer-agent
   metamemory problem: coding agents manage what context
   carries across sessions, but developers have limited
   visibility into what the agent stores, retrieves, and
   applies. MemoSync treats memory as a shared, operable
   representation — the agent proposes memory candidates and
   reports which items it uses; the developer reviews, edits,
   and scopes them. In a study (N=132) participants reached
   higher task performance and the highest memory alignment,
   driven by improved monitoring accuracy, with no increase in
   perceived workload; a week-long deployment with nine
   developers fit everyday work. Open-sourced.
   - **Why it matters for the talk:** "memory you can't see
     is a yes you can't check," as a tested design — and the
     gain came from monitoring accuracy, not from the agent
     getting smarter. Who can see what the worker saw, made
     into a product.
   - **Limits:** coding agents only; alignment measured on
     study tasks; the formative study was 12 developers.
