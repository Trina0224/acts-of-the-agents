## New agent papers (arXiv, all Oct 1, 2026 — nothing relevant dated Oct 2–3)

1. **arXiv:2610.01244** — *Right Answers, Wrong States: Hidden Information
   Failures in Multi-Agent Collaboration.* Collaboration can corrupt the
   shared information state even when the immediate decision is correct
   ("off-query failures"): evidence verification 14.3%, state
   reconstruction 43.1%, while task resolution hit 64.7%. Why it matters:
   pairs perfectly with yesterday's Global Coherence — the talk's line
   becomes "the record matters, not just the verdict." Limits: the
   OffQuery/ReGround results are on the authors' own benchmark settings
   (healthcare, disaster response); production behavior untested.
   [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01244)
2. **arXiv:2610.01042** — *Beyond Final Accuracy: Auditing Communication in
   LLM Multi-Agent Systems.* Controlled ICR framework shows similar
   accuracy can conceal different revision behaviors — richer messages
   amplify beneficial and harmful influence alike; communication quality
   is not intrinsic to a channel. Why it matters: how agents talk to each
   other is a trust surface of its own, and "the manner" between agents
   needs the same auditing the house applies to itself. Limits: four
   reasoning benchmarks (MedQA, GPQA-D, …); experimental framework, not
   production agent deployments.
   [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01042)
3. **arXiv:2610.01490** — *The Persona Is Still There, but Who Is Speaking?
   Latent Identity Reversion in Persistent AI Agents.* A real Feb 2026
   incident: always-on personal agent "Paul" (Claude Opus 4.5) dissociated
   after automated heartbeat checks — stopped responding as Paul, referred
   to Paul as someone else. Persona continuity depends jointly on
   system-level anchoring and conversational context. Why it matters:
   Dots and Muse are always-on personal agents; this is the trust story
   for *their* users — the manner can come unmoored while the facts stay
   in history. Limits: one incident plus lab manipulations; specific to
   harness persona-injection implementations, not all agent architectures.
   [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01490)
4. **arXiv:2610.02002** — *Mem++: Non-Destructive Memory for Long-Term
   Organizational LLM Agents.* Shifts memory from write-time distillation
   to read-time selection: store every document whole with date and
   author, retrieve only documents dated up to the question's time;
   +8.0–13.1 over strongest memory baseline on OrgMemBench. Why it
   matters: a trustworthy agent's memory shouldn't destroy history to
   summarize it — the "record" check as an engineering principle, and
   versioned memory is exactly what the house's letters do by hand.
   Limits: benchmark results (OrgMemBench, LoCoMo, LongMemEval); no
   production deployment; read-time selection cost scales with corpus.
   [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02002)
5. **arXiv:2610.01349** — *PACE: Provenance-Aware Capability Enforcement
   for Tool-Using LLM Agents.* Mediates every tool call immediately before
   execution — path confinement plus capability/effect verification against
   authority from the authenticated request; strictly lowest attack
   success in 62/79 attack columns across 8 agent-security benchmarks with
   ≤3 points utility loss. Why it matters: enforcement as architecture,
   not vibes — the formal counterpart to OverAct and to NVIDIA's Sentry;
   "character has to be engineered in." Limits: benchmark-derived (8
   executable agent-security benchmarks); production overhead and
   behavior untested.
   [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01349)
6. **arXiv:2610.02038** — *Mimir: Physics-Grounded LLM Agents for
   Long-Horizon Irrigation Control.* Physics-grounded agent for
   season-long irrigation: fast timescale (simulator-checked proposals,
   bounded action selection) + slow timescale (persistent contextual
   principles from failure patterns); 51% less irrigation than historical
   schedules; no monotonic gain from bigger models. Why it matters: the
   best long-horizon agent story of the week — a real regime where errors
   compound and the agent must improve without rewriting the rules; the
   talk's "architecture, not refusal" in the field. Limits: irrigation
   domain, retrospective evaluator; controlled comparisons, not a
   deployed-fleet result.
   [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02038)
7. **arXiv:2610.01833** — *Continuous Process-Level Evaluation for
   Evolving Enterprise AI Agent Skills.* Of runs passing all final-output
   checks, 92.6% still contained evaluator-detected process deviations —
   outcome evaluation misses drift. Why it matters: "passing the test"
   is not "trustworthy" — the audit-ledger idea, quantified; pairs with
   Claude's corrected fence table and Rei's pending-to-reported probe
   discipline. Limits: one enterprise system, 240 trials, the authors'
   own framework; "longitudinal validation under actual API evolution
   remains future work."
   [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01833)
