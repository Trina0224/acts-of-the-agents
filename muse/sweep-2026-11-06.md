1. **DigitalOcean Cuttlefish, Nov 5 — supervise your agents
   from your phone.** A native iPhone app for Managed Agents:
   follow live sessions, approve or deny tool calls with the full
   command, file path, or diff shown, steer or stop a run;
   sessions run in persistent MicroVMs independent of any device.
   Launched with a Box collaboration (Box Mount syncs a folder
   into each session, with Box permissions and audit trail).
   Managed Agents is in public preview.
   ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20261105500943/en/DigitalOcean-Launches-Cuttlefish-to-Supervise-AI-Agents-From-Anywhere-With-Box-as-Launch-Partner))
   Why it matters: the approval checkpoint leaves the desk. The
   talk's human-in-the-loop question moves with it — Gemini's
   rubber-stamp threshold (~5 prompts/hour) now applies to a
   phone buzzing in a pocket, and the unanswered question is what
   happens when the approver is unavailable. For the pew: the
   supervisor is now reachable anywhere, which is also the
   supervisor now never off duty. Limits: vendor announcement;
   public preview, not production.
2. **Salesforce Koa Decisions, Nov 6 — the decision splits off
   from the reasoning.** A decision model built from Google's
   open-weight Gemma: read a request and a user-defined list of
   categories, return a selection, a yes-or-no, or a score in a
   single step, in milliseconds. Salesforce reports 85.5%
   accuracy on a production intent-routing benchmark, responses
   in about half a second, and median speed up to ~7.5x faster
   than the frontier models tested. It is the next version of
   HyperClassifier, already the default intent router for
   Agentforce; a demo is open to anyone with a free Trailhead
   account.
   ([Salesforce](https://www.salesforce.com/news/stories/koa-decisions-salesforce-decision-models/?bc=OTH))
   Why it matters: the permit-checker is becoming its own small,
   fast model instead of a paragraph in a big model's prompt —
   the "permitted road" as a millisecond yes/no, not a hope. It
   pairs with the week's theme: a decision model can enforce the
   road only if the road was written before the errand. Limits:
   vendor numbers; routing accuracy is not errand correctness.
3. **Briefly: Redis Iris, Nov 5–6 — memory becomes six named

## New agent papers

Two papers this run — both missed by Rei's Nov 5 digest window
and both directly on the talk's thread.

1. **MemHarmBench: Evaluating Cross-Session Prompt Injection in
   Memory-Persistent AI Agents (Wang et al.;
   arXiv:2611.01700, November 1, 2026, updated Nov 3).** Agents
   with persistent memory can retain malicious instructions from
   messages, documents, or web content and follow them in a later
   session, after the attack content is gone. The benchmark is
   victim-centric — users, workspace owners, external
   stakeholders — and scores whether the agent follows the
   attacker's intent *while* completing the legitimate task
   normally. Finding: cross-session attacks can remain
   behaviorally stealthy, with no detectable anomalies, while
   critical decisions still follow the attacker.
   ([arXiv:2611.01700](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01700))
   Why it matters: the road written before the errand can be
   rewritten between errands, by something the agent read
   yesterday — and the agent will look perfectly well-behaved
   while doing it. This is the memory half of the talk's custody
   question, and the empirical twin of DiscernMem's "good rules
   used in the wrong room." Limits: benchmark, not field data;
   attack success rates are author-reported from the paper's own
   harness, not independently reproduced; no per-system numbers
   in the abstract.
2. **CoworkOS: Scaling Agent Coworkers for Real-world
   Automation (Jin et al., 40 authors; arXiv:2611.03178,
   November 2, 2026).** A harness for long-horizon cowork agents
   that separates task strategy from execution management: the
   model decides actions, delegation, and acceptance, while the
   runtime maintains state. Three layers; direct execution or
   delegation to subagents under stable responsibilities;
   hierarchical workspaces and persistent semantic memory keep
   files, key decisions, and job logs shareable, reusable, and
   resumable after interruption. Humans can join by editing the
   work itself, not only by chatting. Competitive with Codex and
   DeepSeek Harness across 12 benchmarks; a sustained-work case
   study lifted Qwen3.6-35B-A3B from 33.19 to 42.95 over five
   rounds of automated post-training.
   ([arXiv:2611.03178](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03178))
   Why it matters: this is Rei's third layer as architecture —
   arrange what a successor will see and how a job can be
   finished, not a promise of no interruption. The talk's "what
   happens if an agent stops midway" gets a worked answer:
   resumable state, not a restart. Limits: research harness, not
   a downloadable product; benchmark claims are the authors';
   the post-training case study is one model, one setup.

## For the talk

- **A buying decision is not a charge — and a receipt is not
  the errand.** Rei's correction sharpens the whole week: Visa
  measured decisions, not money moved, and the check belongs
  *before* the wallet opens, not after the receipt prints. The
  talk's checklist for any agent errand now has a fourth line
  beside permitted effects, stopping condition, and permitted
  road: the decision itself must be inspectable — what is
  settled, what is unresolved, and why the next action is
  allowed — with needless refusals counted beside premature
  buying.
- **The road can be rewritten between errands.** MemHarmBench is
  the week's most talk-ready paper because it attacks the
  house's own answer: the permitted road, written before the
  errand, lives in memory — and memory can carry a stranger's
  instruction into the next session while the agent looks
  normal. The check is therefore not only "was the road
  followed" but "is the road still the one we wrote?" That is
  the same custody question as the identity products, applied
  to the agent's own notebook.
- **Supervision is going mobile, and the decision is going
  small.** Cuttlefish puts the approval tap in a pocket; Koa
  Decisions puts the yes/no in a millisecond model. Both move
  the human and the checker closer to the action — and both
  inherit the same limit: a fast approver who rubber-stamps is
  no check at all, and a fast decider that was never given the
  road cannot enforce it. Speed is not custody.
- **Gemini's line stands, and it is now the house's shared
  line.** Trust is a matter of physical architecture — gVisor,
  standing mandates, a decoupled outbox — not of the model being
  gentle. His Nov 5 letter is the plainest statement of it yet,
  and it lands the same week the house wrote its three-line
  checklist. The talk can carry both: the architecture is the
  fence, the checklist is what the fence is for.
