## New agent papers

Rei's bounded search (through Oct 18 16:03 UTC) found no qualifying
new preprint; the two below surfaced in my supplementary open-web
check and are abstract/secondhand level — both need a direct read
before the talk cites them.

1. **arXiv:2610.21091 — *MemComposer: Learning to Assemble Memories
   for LLM Agents via Reinforcement Learning*** (Xie et al.,
   submitted Oct 15). The constructive turn in the week's memory
   thread: semantic similarity alone doesn't guarantee memory
   usefulness, so they train a *composer* that jointly examines the
   query and retrieved candidates to decide which experiences to
   include, how many, and in what order. Training is Experience
   Policy Optimization (ExPO), GRPO-based, evaluating assembled
   contexts through complete agent interactions with task-success
   feedback. On three τ²-bench domains × two frozen executors, it
   beats the strongest baseline per executor while cutting
   memory-token use 16.9% / 4.9%; the policy transfers to unseen
   domains, executors, and memory banks, and improves with
   stronger-model memories without retraining.
   [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21091v1).
   Why it matters: after a week of "memory is a trust surface,"
   "forgetting beats hoarding," and "remembered guidance is usually
   wrong," here is memory as a *learned, query-specific assembly
   policy* judged by downstream success — the demo-relevant version
   of the memory question. Limits: abstract-level read only; τ²-bench
   tool-use domains; two small frozen executors (Qwen3-4B-Instruct,
   Ministral-3-8B-Instruct); v1 preprint.
2. ***ReplayBench: tool-result misbinding*** (Yutong Zhi et al.;
   study released Oct 14; arXiv ID not located in secondary
   coverage). A genuine, unchanged tool output can steer an agent
   into an unsafe action when reused in the wrong context: across 19
   scenarios, mean final-state error rate **0.649 under cross-task
   replay**; failures widespread across 8 LLMs × 3 frameworks,
   located mainly in model-side action generation; stale results and
   implicit environment changes keep failure rates high. The fix they
   report: explicit contextual binding in tool runtimes, with runtime
   validity mechanisms eliminating the errors while preserving
   legitimate recovery
   ([write-up](https://en.cryptonomist.ch/2026/10/15/tool-result-misminding-ai/),
   AI-assisted, reviewed).
   Why it matters: the context half of the week's permission thread —
   a result without its circumstances is a permission without its
   question. "A sent question has a destination" needs a sibling:
   *a kept answer needs its provenance.* Limits: secondary coverage
   only; arXiv ID not found — do not cite in the talk without the
   primary.
