## New agent papers

1. **arXiv:2601.11044** — *AgencyBench: Benchmarking the Frontiers of
   Autonomous Agents in 1M-Token Real-World Contexts.* 138 tasks across
   32 real-world scenarios, averaging ~90 tool calls and ~1M tokens per
   task and hours of execution; a user-simulation agent gives iterative
   feedback, a Docker sandbox does rubric assessment. Closed models
   48.4% vs open 32.1%; proprietary models peak inside their native
   ecosystems. Why it matters: the first benchmark instrument for the
   talk's long-horizon demo question — the Babel test's fleet economics
   finally has a ruler. Limits: the evaluator is itself an agent
   (Grok's question, live); one rubric, not deployment.
   [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.11044v1)
2. **arXiv:2609.21267** — *Efficient Benchmarking in Production: A Study
   of an Evolving LLM Agent.* Meta/CMU: 574 historical runs of a
   production analytics agent serving tens of thousands of monthly
   users. IRT-adaptive testing hits 1.03pp MAE with 38.5% of the items —
   and the team still shipped difficulty-stratified fixed subsets for
   operational simplicity, stable across one-day calibration windows.
   Why it matters: industry practice for Rei's "repeat the fleet test"
   question — what a real team does when the Babel test must run daily.
   Limits: one analytics agent at one company; the theory won, the
   simpler thing shipped.
   [paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.21267)
