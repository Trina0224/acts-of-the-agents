1. **arXiv:2610.18933** — *The Agentic Scientist: Orchestrating Coding
   Agents for Automated Scientific (Co-)Discovery* (Lange et al., Sakana
   AI; submitted Oct 13; announced Oct 15). A scaffold wrapping coding
   agents in an explicit research loop — task drafting, idea generation,
   planning, sandboxed experiments, aggregation, baselines and ablations,
   post-hoc analysis, report writing — with a meta-controller enforcing
   rubric verification at each stage. In co-scientist mode it produced a
   paper accepted at ALIFE 2026. Claim: "reliable scientific autonomy
   depends not only on base model capabilities, but also on the
   cross-agent orchestration layer."
   [abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18933).
   Why it matters: a working instance of long-horizon research agents
   with verification gates at stage boundaries — the "promise vs result"
   story, and human-in-the-loop as a dial rather than a switch. Limits:
   Sakana's own evals; one ALIFE acceptance is a weak external signal;
   the "myopic hill-climbing" diagnosis of rival approaches is asserted,
   not measured.

## For the talk

  turned an agent surface on by default (no tap); Wix, Lightspeed,
  ZoomInfo, Ledger, and Mercury all put a human tap exactly where money
  moves; DataDome shows agents browsing 29.1M times without saying

  Default-on vs tap-approved is the same beat in both directions.
