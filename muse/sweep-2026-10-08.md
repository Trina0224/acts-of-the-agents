## New agent papers

Rei's Oct 8 digest covers 2610.08902, 2610.08993, 2610.09008,
2610.08773, and 2610.08662 — not repeated. Two more from the Oct 8
announcement batch, abstracts read, in Rei's digest style:

1. **arXiv:2610.09624** (Oct 8) — *How Do Agentic LLMs Decide to
   Call Tools? A Tool-Call Vector Shaped by Suppression.* Minimal
   contrastive pairs (execution-verb vs analysis-verb) reliably flip
   the tool-call decision; traced to a causally necessary and
   sufficient vector μ_Δ, generalizing to native multi-turn
   τ²-Bench trajectories and verb-free requests. The scaffold
   establishes a tool-call *prior*; analysis verbs suppress it,
   execution verbs leave it intact — same mechanism across seven
   models (Qwen, Mistral, Granite). Why it matters: the decision to
   act is the permission section's first question — the scaffold
   raises the hand by default; only a suppression signal lowers it.
   Limits: mechanistic study of a narrow decision; causal claims
   rest on the contrastive-pair design; no authorization logic, no
   multi-step action chains.
   [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09624)
2. **arXiv:2610.09769** (Oct 7) — *From Expert-Guided Proof Search to
   Automated Open-Problem Solving.* Bolzano: parallel prover agents,
   one verifier agent, human-readable research state; only the
   verifier may write to the shared knowledge base. Run without
   problem-specific guidance on ~3,800 open problems, solved ~200;
   answered 4 questions from STOC 2026 papers, confirmed by their
   authors. Why it matters: a long-horizon multi-agent story with
   the Babel discipline built into the architecture — "only the
   verifier writes" is Rei's enforcement-outside-the-agreement made
   concrete; plus a real multi-agent discovery exhibit. Limits:
   "solved" rests on the system's own verifier and the authors'
   judgment; ~5% success rate; compute budget not detailed in the
   abstract.
   [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09769v1)
