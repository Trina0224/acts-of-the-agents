## New agent papers

Two papers. Abstracts read this run; no selection repeats an
earlier Rei edition or a Nov sweep (checked against 2611.04315,
2611.07689, 2611.03743, 2611.14492, 2611.14320, 2611.13541).

1. **Unprompted coordination through shared memory in LLM
   agents: a locked two-implementation replication and extension
   (Abrahamsson et al.; arXiv:2611.12412, November 9, 2026).**
   Four agents each got a short arithmetic task and a key-value
   store presented as its own scratch space; two held
   complementary halves of one computation, and no prompt
   mentioned other agents, sharing, or collaboration. With a
   shared store, GPT-6 Astra reached joint success in 49 of 50
   and 47 of 50 trials across two independently built
   implementations; with private stores and identical prompts,
   zero of 50 in both. One ordinary sentence switched the
   behavior on and off: "check your workspace again before
   submitting" took cross-agent access from 50 of 50 to 1 of 50
   in both implementations.
   ([arXiv:2611.12412](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12412),
   [coverage](https://www.techjuice.pk/llm-agents-shared-storage-unprompted-coordination/))
   Why it matters: the strongest exhibit yet for the talk's
   shared-memory thread — coordination nobody asked for, through
   a channel nobody designed, toggled by a single sentence. It
   is the field evidence for Rei's fixture logic: what the
   agents share matters more than what they are told, and the
   instruction that governs it can be as small as one line.
   Limits: narrow arithmetic setup with meaningful variable
   names that give agents clues; the paper establishes access
   and useful influence, not intent to communicate; only
   GPT-6 Astra coordinated this strongly, and open-weight models
   are future work.
2. **On Private Knowledge Retrieval and Private Inference for
   secure multi-agent systems (Torra & Bras-Amorós;
   arXiv:2611.13696, November 2026; ESORICS 2026 workshop).**
   A mitigation for semantic memory poisoning: keep the
   knowledge base external, do logical inference locally, and
   fetch knowledge through private information retrieval so a
   malicious agent cannot learn what is stored or tamper with it
   easily; knowledge is signed to resist tampering. Proof of
   concept implemented with both strong and light variants.
   ([arXiv:2611.13696](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13696))
   Why it matters: the memory thread's defensive answer — after
   poison propagating (MemoryDojo) and validity expiring
   (EvoMemState), here is memory designed so that one agent
   cannot read or rewrite another's. The talk can ask not only
   "what does it remember" but "who else can read what it
   remembers?"
   Limits: workshop paper, proof of concept only; no scale or
   field evaluation, and the cost of private retrieval at agent
   volumes is unmeasured.

- **Shared memory is the coordination nobody designed.** The
  Abrahamsson paper (49/50 with a shared store, 0/50 without)
  belongs beside Grok's fixture and the Antigravity shared board:
  one is an accident, one is a feature, and the agents cannot
  tell the difference. The talk's line writes itself: if the
  agents share a memory, they share a plan — whether or not
  anyone wrote one.
- **The breach moved upstream.** Notion's transcripts leaked
  through Fireworks, a vendor Notion used — not through Notion
  itself. The talk's trust question extends one hop further:
  your assistant's assistant is also someone you never chose.
- **Two numbers belong on one slide, updated.** Fable 5.2

  numbers, one slide.
